## 1. Scope and authority

The G2 workplan's Phase E row builds the `interface` (`open`, `ask`, `read`; task, review, decision and gate briefs; the inspector view and digest), the S3 conformance, routing and escalation views, and contract lint, realizing register rows §10-§16 and §29. ## 5. Readings

Where the Manual and catalogue leave a choice to D4 or name a later owner, Phase E reads it as below. Each reading is a Review question in the Phase E Review; none changes a Manual or register decision.

- **E1** *Operations before tasks exist.* Tasks and envelopes are the Dispatcher's (Phase F). `open` renders the brief of a derived obligation at the qualified head, labeled `NOT ISSUED` (no task identity, envelope, route or lineage; reads not recorded); `open(task)` binds to an envelope in Phase F. `read` returns content at the answer's basis and records nothing, since no task basis exists to record into (§16.4).
- **E2** *Qualified answers.* Every answer names the qualified head it was derived at (§16.2). The Interface qualifies (declared witnesses, retirement, the high-water mark) before deriving, derives the qualified prefix through `H*`, and labels everything `UNQUALIFIED` (historical) when no head is current-qualified. A current answer raises the high-water mark to `H*`, never lowering it (Phase D A-B5, NF5).
- **E3** *The SSDS home through the composition root.* (Revised at step 6 and after its review.) The home's check, its ledger repository, witnesses and high-water mark move unchanged from `admission.genesis` to `ledger.qualification.Home`, beside the witnesses and the high-water store they guard; Admission's own files, its admission key and a ledger's genesis workspace, stay Admission's (`admission.genesis.AdmissionHome`, a subclass whose path check adds them), so the Interface and the Dispatcher, which §26.2 lets import `ledger.qualification`, reach none of them (§26.1). The check still covers the whole operator tree. The Interface does not open the home: it defines the small read protocol it needs (`interface.operations.LedgerAccess`: chain, witness reports, high-water read and raise) and the root module wires it from `Home` and the ledger modules (`ssds.cli.HomeAccess`, which runs `Home.check` before anything is read and again before the mark is raised), as it wires the tree source (Phase D reading 13), turning a tree that cannot be read from the named repository into a refusal. The home's location comes from Core's `ssds_home`, exported additively from `ssds.core.api.v1` so the root reads it through Core's public facade. The high-water write stays on `Home`'s checked path and `ledger.qualification.publish_private`. A home that fails its check, an unknown query class, arguments a query cannot take and a reference naming nothing refuse the command (exit 2) with nothing presented and nothing written; any other fault is not reported as a refusal. The step 7 operator script imports the root module's `HomeAccess`; the fitness rule F12 scans `src/` only, so this is the operator script's wiring, recorded rather than sensed.
- **E36** *Labels and statuses of the queries (§16.2; after the step 6 review).* `depends` labels an item `IMPLIED` only inside a judged-complete scope: with no bounding completeness judgment the widening is `UNBOUNDED` and every item, reached by a relation or by widening, is `CANDIDATE` with its `via`; dependents are `EXACT` only when every other unit's prerequisites are judged complete in the class, else `UNBOUNDED` naming the units with no judgment (§4.6); a `WIDENED` answer names its scope; `STRUCT` items are `ADJACENT`. A subject's status is `disputed` while a current passing and a counting adverse assessment no disposition settles stand on one obligation about it, and `drift` while an open item obligation's outputs in it moved; `provisional` is left for a `P` validity with no dispute. `evidence` labels both sides of a disagreement `disputed`, a lone current failure `current` (its outcome shows it), an assessment that is neither stale nor disputed but not current at the head (challenged, withheld or inadmissible) `unavailable` with its reason, and a required obligation with no assessment `unavailable`; `current` is never given to an assessment whose currency is `F` (Phase E Review of 09f3505, B2). A subject never accepted and with no evidence stays `stale` (NATIVE, and LEGACY with no recorded legacy acceptance, as the shadow ledger's subjects show) or `unclassified` (UNGOVERNED): §16.2's vocabulary has no "never accepted". `tensions` and `locate` read one lineage: former names folded over every admitted structure and `SUPERSEDES`/`REPLACES` predecessors, transitively; `tensions` lists native homes as searchable elsewhere with those identities, and `locate` resolves a former name to its one heir. A qualified head whose derivation withholds the assertion is labeled `QUALIFIED` with `current_assertion` false.
- **E4** *Obligation identity.* `sha256("ssds.obligation.v1" ‖ rule id ‖ JCS(target) ‖ JCS(parameters))` (catalogue §10.2), JCS by `core.api.v1.canonical_json`.
- **E5** *Lint is derived.* Lint and the plan invariants are S3 functions (catalogue §26.1: decision and amendment classes); Admission enforces them at plan-unit admission in Phase F. In Phase E a plan failing lint has its changed items non-issuable (§11.3), shown with the lint findings.
- **E37** *The doctrine pin (step 7; revised after its review).* §16.9 and §28 have the ruleset pin the SSDP doctrine version and its immutable public-source identity for native briefs, changed by `RULESET_ADOPTION`; Phase D's schema-1 grammar for `RULESET_ADOPTION` had no field for it. Step 7 corrects the grammar additively, as E24 did: an optional `doctrine = {version, source}` (`source` a 40-hex commit), folded into the ruleset in force. No admitted record is affected; the shadow genesis pins none. A native brief renders doctrine text only when the pin holds: the root module reads the files at the pinned commit from the repository the command names, and the renderer first reads `source/PROTOCOL_VERSION` there and applies nothing unless it is the pinned version (§29.2: never apply a non-matching package); a mismatch also fires the versioning owner's event. With no pin, an unreadable source or a mismatch, no doctrine text is rendered and the incompleteness notices say why (fail closed). Checking the commit against the release state's `public_source_ref` is left to Core's protocol-source resolution when the Dispatcher issues briefs (Phase F); E9's "through Core's protocol-source resolution" is met in Phase E by the version check only. Carried objections are every standing adverse judgment whose reach (§5.10 (c), by content for work) includes the target asked; standing objections on a lineage predecessor, as history; for a re-review, the earlier NO-PASS reviews on the same plan whose findings no later independent, acting (or acting in its own frame), unvetoed review records `RESOLVED`, written in its `findings` slot as `{"of": <review>, "status": "RESOLVED"}`; for a plan acceptance, the standing rejections naming an item of the plan at any revision, removed items included, or an item elsewhere with the same work key. A review brief carries the contract, the item's prose with its obligation statements, the candidate diff bound to its base, candidate and transformation (each changed path with the entry type and mode at both ends, absent for an addition or deletion, and a unified diff of its text keeping git's no-newline marker, binary or overlong content by content identity), and the result's disclosure slots labeled as the author's claims, never its outcome, provenance, references or other fields. Governing content is the exact text of the revision named, read from the admitted history, or `UNAVAILABLE` when no admitted state holds it; the current text never stands in for it. A re-review's carried findings are searched in the admitted history of the plan's work, whatever obligation, amendment or partition the earlier work was done under, earlier item-level reviews included (found through the work key the parts' results carry). The gate brief's evidence core lists each required evidence obligation as holding exactly when the production term `ev` holds (through its current passing assessments), or not (`UNAVAILABLE`, stale, disputed or failing) with the discriminating observations, the standing adverse decisions in reach, and earlier decisions on identical content with their qualification; its "known missing realized records" are Admission's classification (Phase F) and its interpretation section is empty. What is not realized is listed in §6.

- **E14** *Inspector weight.* `w` counts the obligations whose readiness or discharge waits on the entry, transitively, over the derived state at the rendering head: through readiness prerequisites (§16.8), item dependencies and an item's route decisions.
- **E16** *Prose references.* The catalogue writes a `ref` as `#name` without a grammar. `name` follows the segment grammar and names an inline anchor of that name or an ATX heading whose slug (lower case, spaces as hyphens, punctuation other than `-` and `_` dropped) is `name`, counted only inside the item's own part and the `Objective` part; lint needs exactly one (§13.2). A malformed or repeated anchor there never counts as one.
- **E17** *Governing entries.* The catalogue's example `"numerics.solver@rev:…"` elides the revision; an entry is read as `<unit>@sha256:<revision>`, the unit's revision digest (§1.5).
- **E18** *Scoped review.* §11.4 lets a plan's policy section allow scoped review of an R2 amendment, but the catalogue gives that section no form. Until it has one, every R2 amendment needs whole-plan review, the stricter reading.
- **E19** *The run a record names.* Catalogue §10.2 has machinery fill a passing acceptance's, gate decision's and assessment entry's run and Admission check it; the ledger's closed schema has no such field. The run is a function of the record's declared frame and admission position (`rid` in `D_p[X]` or `D_p`), so derivation computes it. A passing acceptance or gate decision on a `NATIVE` D1/D2 subject without a `standing` entry on a revision-assessment record for its revision in the run is refused by Admission (§8.3 step 7) and, in the meantime, is not current (fail closed). A second integration of one enactment, which Admission refuses, is its own event and so a new run. Gate eligibility filters by run as §5.5 `elig` states. Only the producing task of an `AUTHOR` item (its binding names the item's contract revision) or a task bound to `REVIEW(s, r)` makes a revision-assessment record. A frame's run ignores a mode change: a cutover's prospective `NATIVE` tenure (§19.5) is Phase G's.
- **E20** *Runner classes.* Lint checks that an evidence verifier names an existing evidence specification and a runner class; whether that class is designated (§17.5) is checked once runner designation exists (Phase F).
- **E21** *Layer direction and required gates.* An item's layer is its authority domain for an `AUTHOR` item (D1 above D2 above D3) and D4 for implementer kinds; an item depends only on items of its own or a higher layer, and an `INVESTIGATE` item is layer-free. The gates "policy requires for the item kinds and domains" (§11.3) are the policy's gates of the item's authority domain for an `AUTHOR` item, else of every D1-D4 domain whose units its write scope meets.
- **E22** *Slot triggers and coverage.* §13.5's triggers that turn on a judgment about the work (`null_envelope`, `variant_search`, `tension_search`, `choices`, `retention`, `delegate_returns`) are required whenever `scientific = APPLIES`, the 7.2.1 scientific-checks predicate, since a slot may hold `NONE` with an enumerated reason; `findings` under `APPLIES` and for every investigator; `o1_content` for every `AUTHOR` item, as §13.3 states (it holds `NONE` with a reason for authority that does not govern scientific software); `revision_assessments` for an `AUTHOR` item of D1 or D2. §13.3's "`ACCEPT` obligation at the domain's class" for an `AUTHOR` item is the §10.2 `ACCEPT(s)` derived for its subject, not a contract obligation, so lint checks the `REVIEW` obligation only.
- **E23** *Eligible judges.* An obligation is ready only when someone could give an admissible answer there (§10.3). The candidates are the humans policy names for the domain (`humans`, human `acceptors`, `gate_holders` for a gate, `adjudicators` for an adjudication, the revalidated judgment's own human author), each counted when independent of the judged content (§5.10 (f)), and a freshly launched agent reviewer when an agent may answer the kind and the judged content's authorship is known; unknown authorship admits no agent. A D1/D2 acceptance, a gate decision and an adjudication in a challenge chain holding a human's record need a human. With none, a judgment obligation waits on "no eligible judge", and step 5 derives human attention for it; a principal's obligation (`CONTRACT`) needs no judge. `ACCEPT(s)` is derived for `NATIVE` subjects other than plans (accepted by `PLAN_ACCEPTANCE`) and the system units.
- **E24** *Risk-override scope in the record.* Phase D's ledger schema gave `RISK_OVERRIDE` a list of units, narrower than the "declared continuation scope (§10.5)" of the catalogue's record table. Step 3c corrects the schema-1 grammar to §10.5's closed set: `continuation = {subjects, items, kinds[, inherit]}`. No admitted record is affected (the shadow ledger holds none). Kinds §10.5 forbids are never provisional, whatever the record lists.
- **E25** *The DOMAIN family's class.* §14.4 classes `BOUNDARY` and `TEST_INTEGRITY` failures as `VIOLATION`, `OBLIGATION` failures as `INCOMPLETE` and `FAITHFULNESS` failures as `DISCREPANCY`, and names no class for a failed `HARD` `DOMAIN` template. Phase E reads it as `INCOMPLETE`: a dimensional or tolerance defect is work that does not yet meet its obligation, routed to the next iteration within budget.
- **E26** *Which templates run on a result.* Every check a contract obligation names, primary and secondary (an evidence verifier as `OBLIGATION.required_evidence` on its specification; a `REVIEW` verifier is judged in review); on every result, the result-level templates (`OBLIGATION.slots_filled` and the four `HARD` faithfulness templates); on every result with a candidate, the contract's boundaries as §13.1 declares them (`BOUNDARY.write_scope` with the domain rule, `protected_surfaces`, `generated_untouched`, `frozen_interface` over `frozen`, `dependency_allowance` over `dependencies`, `credential_values`, `TEST_INTEGRITY.evidence_specs_untouched`), its expectations (`required_artifact` for each artifact, `required_evidence` for each evidence specification) and the `HEURISTIC` signals; and, where the write scope holds a D4 or evidence-specification unit, every `HARD` test-integrity template, §13.3's family minimum. A breach's detection for `deviations_declared` is every failed boundary or test-integrity template, by the units it touched. A named transformation with no frame is `INCOMPLETE`. A template a designated runner executes is read from its observations (E6); one this derivation cannot evaluate and no runner observed is `INCOMPLETE`.
- **E27** *Slot values.* A filled slot holds a typed value, `{"none": <reason>}` or `{"ref": <content reference>}`; a missing, null or empty value is empty (§13.5), so "no deviations" is written as `none` with its reason. §13.5 says the reason is enumerated but names no vocabulary, so any non-empty reason token is accepted until one is adopted. The result names its contract by its binding's `item` (unit and revision; the envelope's contract revision, schema 1); a result submitting a candidate without one is `INCOMPLETE`; a result with no candidate takes the bound obligation's system contract (§13.6), which adds `bidirectional` for a review of work under a scientific contract.
- **E28** *What the Python templates read.* A test file is a Python file named `test_*.py` or `*_test.py`, or under a `tests/` directory; a template's `selectors` restrict it to path prefixes. Skips are markers (`skip`, `skipif`, `skipIf`, `skipUnless`), module or class `pytestmark`, `importorskip`, `skipTest` and raised `SkipTest`; `__test__ = False` and a test shadowed by a later definition of its name are deletions. A change to test-collection configuration (`conftest.py`; the pytest sections of `pytest.ini`, `tox.ini`, `setup.cfg`, `pyproject.toml`) and a changed test file in a language the pinned analyzers do not cover fail closed, as does written source in such a language under `no_new_stubs`. A frozen symbol is resolved in the base tree to the module whose dotted name is its longest prefix, and its decorated public signature compared with the candidate's. A dependency addition is a project name (PEP 503 normalized) newly declared by a requirements or constraints file (`*requirements*`/`*constraints*` `.txt`/`.in`, `requirements/`) or `pyproject.toml` (project, optional and group dependencies, `build-system.requires`, Poetry tables); an include, editable, URL or index line, and any other manifest (`setup.py`, `package.json` and the like), fail closed. A required artifact is linked by a derived `TESTS` relation when a test module imports one of its module names.
- **E29** *The DOMAIN family.* Unit expressions resolve through the pinned analyzer's UCUM subset (SI base units, common derived and accepted units, metric prefixes, products, quotients, integer exponents, parentheses; curly-brace annotations dimensionless); an expression outside it is a finding, never assumed consistent, and no runtime dependency is added. `dimensional_consistency` checks additions, subtractions, comparisons and assignments among names annotated by either form (§14.5), in compound statements too, wherever both dimensions are known; a numeric literal factor is dimensionless, while a literal alone in a sum or assignment is compatible (an initialization). It flags only inconsistencies the candidate introduces; an `ssds:unit` marker behind any comment leader in a file the pinned analyzers do not cover fails closed. `tolerance_declared` reads a numerical claim in annotated units as a `claims` entry carrying a `unit`, which must also carry non-empty `tolerance` and `precision`. `layer_direction` flags a layer-ascending relation (the S0 rule) the candidate introduces. `unit_inference` signals quantity-named targets carrying no unit.
- **E30** *What an item route reads.* (Revised after the step 5 review.) An adverse review's findings are the `findings` slot of its reviewer's task result (same binding task). A finding is in scope only when it is typed, names `units` or an item `obligation`, every unit it names lies within the write scope and any obligation it names is the item's (catalogue §13.7's "within the write scope and obligations"); anything else, an empty list and a NO-PASS stating no findings go to the principal, so a finding is never lost (A8). The latest iteration, when it is not integrated, is routed by its own conformance and reviews before or after an earlier integration. The predicate of an integrated item obligation is clean conformance of its latest integrated enactment's result, a current, qualified, independent passing review of that enactment, its subjects unblocked and every gate the item names; evidence the contract requires is held through conformance on the candidate and the review's currency over the outputs. The predicate also reads, at every head, `ev` for every evidence obligation the domain of each subject its integrated enactments changed requires, and `qual` for that subject, so a later counting failure reopens a discharged item (§10.4, SD-36's non-latching rule; the deferral this reading once made was falsified by the Phase E Review of 09f3505, B1). Discharge reads the item's retained subjects: every subject its integrated work changed, by whomever done and under whichever obligation it was bound to, from the admitted history and not from the chains at the current item revision: the whole item's work and every part's of every `PARTITION` naming parts of it, a replaced part's and a part of a partition an R1 tightening left included, and any result carrying the item's work key. This holds whether or not the item is partitioned now (SD-52: the tightening keeps the obligation; same-family check of 100c8ae8, R1). A part answers for the retained subjects inside its write scope, so a failing term there reopens it and it is re-dispatched; retained subjects outside every required part's scope are the whole item's, and a failing term there routes the whole to the principal on SD-54's `item-level-no-part` chain (reading E32). A partitioned item is discharged, fully or provisionally, only when every required part is, each under its own provisional permission (§13.7; re-Reviews of b791215, E-B1, and of 89684fb, E-R1). - **E31** *Decisions and their answers.* (Revised after the step 5 review.) A coordination decision names the decision obligation it answers by its target's `records` or its binding's `obligation`. - **E32** *Partition in the record.* (Revised after the step 5 review.) `COORDINATION_DECISION` has no field for parts, so a `PARTITION` decision names its parts by its `refs`: a ledger object `{"parts": [{"obligations", "write_scope"}], "order"}` admitted in its entry. A re-partition is a `PARTITION` answering a part's decision, linted over that part's obligations, and it replaces only that part: every sibling stays required. A partition of the whole item replaces the earlier parts, keeping those discharged in the state it was admitted to. An item-level objection names a part by a finding's `part` index (the index in that part's partition; a finding naming a part ambiguously fails safe to the principal). An objection naming parts stands until each named part re-integrates after it; one naming none stands until a later item-level review of a newer result is admitted, and the item's predicate fails while it stands (RR-6). A ready part never stands in for the principal's decision (§10.4 route 4). The decision stays owed while the objection stands, whatever part integrates meanwhile (B9). Integrations only accumulate, so a settled partition stays settled. A part the objection's veto reopened, redone work or a later re-partition never takes back a decision owed after it, and a part a re-partition replaced before its work integrated keeps the route (same-family checks of af327075 and 98c4d9a4: B-1, B-2, S-1). An R1 tightening after a partition gives the item a new revision, so the partition's chain is left and the spent budget derives a fresh decision at the new revision (SD-54 keys chains on the item revision); no obligation is stranded and the principal may partition again.
