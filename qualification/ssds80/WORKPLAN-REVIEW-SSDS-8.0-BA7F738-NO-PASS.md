## SERIOUS CHALLENGE

**None against accepted governing authority.** The [kernel][kernel] says: “A coherent parent with a wrong child is an ordinary blocker.” The governing [evidence owner][evidence] is coherent and realizable here. It keeps the evidentiary target separate from execution dependency (“implementation, harness, fixture, dataset, environment, backend, tool or other machinery required to realize/interpret it”), lists applicability dimensions that include “evidence-spec revision, subject/candidate, governed-claim revision … oracle semantics, environment/backend/precision/configuration”, and says:

- “A valid failing observation is evidence. A stale failing observation is not admissible evidence against current authority. A stale passing observation is not admissible confirmation.”
- “When competent evidence assessments materially conflict, preserve the disagreement and route adjudication to the governing claim/owner or obtain discriminating evidence. … supersession/adjudication must be explicit enough to distinguish resolved reassessment from still-live disagreement. An unresolved material disagreement keeps dependent present guidance/closure visibly qualified.”
- “An assembled outcome after several simultaneous changes cannot be attributed to one mechanism without discriminating evidence.”

The three-clause family rule revision 19 adopts (start only on an admitted fact or in the record's own lineage; stop only by a judgment or by evidence that discriminates in the observations; a stop earned by supersession stands on immutable facts) is a sound concretization of that owner. Both blockers are places where the candidate's definitions do not meet it, and both lie in D3.

## Verdict

**NO-PASS.** Two blockers remain. Both are new members of the family the previous Review named, “what a failing assessment's adversity depends on”, and both sit in the content notions revision 19 introduced or kept:

- **U1 — “the same content” is decided by the named evidentiary premises, which are narrower than what a realization executes.** Condition 4, the rerun trigger and `here` compare only the claim, the specification unit and the execution content the specification named; condition 5 and the withdrawal latch inherit that comparison. A candidate that changes execution content outside those premises (a runtime parameter file, data read by path, dynamically loaded code, build flags; under a declared-only reading of “named”, any helper module) is treated as if it ran on admitted content. Then (U1a) a candidate failure caused by such content counts in the admitted state at once, with no rerun obligation and no pair possible, so only an owner judgment (R3 for D1/D2) lifts it, and it blocks its own lineage even after the fix and every other lineage that requires the obligation; and (U1b) any candidate whose run of a pair's configuration gives another evaluated outcome through such content, even a passing one, permanently withdraws a standing pair in every frame, resurrects the resolved failure in the admitted state, and forbids a new pair on admitted content. The design's stated guarantees “A candidate failure about candidate code therefore costs one pair” and “The pair therefore never lapses through mere change” are false on these trajectories.
- **U2 — failures caused by never-admitted interpretive content reach the admitted state with no mechanical disposition.** The start latch keys on obligation identity, and an evidence obligation is unit-granular (`EVIDENCES` targets “the unit holding the claim”). A frame that tightens the oracle or tolerance of an existing check, or revises or adds claim text inside an existing evidenced unit, produces a failing assessment that counts in the admitted state at once. The evaluated outcome on admitted content is identical, so condition 2 can never hold, and the never-admitted-specification exception covers only outputs the trial lacks. The admitted subject stays qualified after the frame is abandoned until an owner judgment (R3 for D1/D2). This is `4ec0024` C2 reproduced through the interpretive dimension, and §6.6's C2b sentence holds only for claims placed in new units.

Revision 19 closes `4ec0024` B′1 and B′2 on their trajectories, closes C1 on its trajectory, and closes C2a, C2c (for never-admitted obligations) and C2b (for new units). It closes N5, N6 and N7, and closes N2, N3, N4 and N8 in part. Per the [convergence owner][convergence] (“post-simplification recurrence, or evidence governing abstraction is wrong -> bounded reconsideration of the earliest affected abstraction”), this record routes U1 and U2 as one family: revision 19 reads three different notions of what a failure is about (obligation identity for start, named premises for stop and standing, specification and claim revisions for the reading), and each defect sits where two of them disagree. The repair directions below are narrow rule changes, not new mechanisms.

Requested checks: the four routing tests pass and `git diff --check` is clean. The model check is green at depths 4 and 5: 41/41 discriminators, with 317,340 and 13,039,519 transitions, matching §0.1. Every sensitivity mode (`REV18`, `REV17`, `REV16`, `REV14`, `REV13`, `REV12`, `REV11`, `REV10`) is red on owner-grounded properties, not only on the oracle. The model has one monotone content counter, binary outcomes and no frames, so “same premises, different executed content”, a revert, “another outcome that still fails”, claim revisions and frame lineages are unrepresentable in it; it cannot see U1 or U2. None of these checks establishes semantic adequacy.

## 1. Blockers by earliest owner

### U1 — premise identity stands in for executed content in the pair, the rerun trigger, `here` and the withdrawal latch

**Owner text at the candidate.**

- §6.6 dimension table: evidentiary premises are “governed-claim content; the specification unit's own content; execution content the specification named when the realization was admitted”, “recorded at admission as content identities”; “everything else in the assessment's basis” is **provenance**: “it can end the assessment's currency, never its adversity”.
- §6.6, rerun trigger: “When the recorded evidentiary premises of a failing assessment `a-` that counts in a frame, and has no disposition standing there, differ from that frame's content, S3 derives a **rerun obligation**”.
- §6.6, condition 4: “**A premise changed.** The content evaluated by the reproduction and by the trial differs in at least one recorded evidentiary premise.”
- §6.6, condition 5: “every admitted observation of the configuration on `a-`'s content has `a-`'s evaluated outcome; every admitted observation of it on the trial's content has the trial's”. Rationale: “A failure whose configuration has ever produced another evaluated outcome on either content is nondeterministic in fact, and goes to the owner”.
- §6.6, withdrawal latch: “A later admitted observation or failing assessment that would have failed condition 5 withdraws the pair's standing (S0, monotone: once withdrawn, always withdrawn). `a-` then counts again … and no pair can be formed again on that content.”
- §6.6, `here_G(p)`: “some admitted observation of p's configuration on G's content has an evaluated outcome other than the failing one, and none has the failing one … ‘on G's content’: every evidentiary premise it recorded has the same content identity in G”.
- §6.6, custody: “No pair is authorized while an execution of its configuration on either content is outstanding.”
- §6.6, start: “For an obligation already admitted, the ordinary case, that is at once, including a candidate's failure of an admitted test”; “A candidate failure about candidate code therefore costs one pair, and one that is also about admitted content stays disputed.” Standing: “The pair therefore never lapses through mere change, and never hides a failure that the current content reproduces.”
- §6.6 decision class and §7.9 floor: dispositions on D1/D2 subjects are R3 except the two pair cases; the floor item “is created at once where S3 derived no rerun obligation for it”.
- §27.32: “Another lineage's failure on an admitted obligation | Blocks until its pair on admitted content disposes of it everywhere”.
- §26 already lists, as a rate trigger, “genuine fixes land in exercised code the specification does not name (dynamic dispatch, an analyzer's fallback)”. It does not cover failures or observations of *never-admitted candidate* content.

**Trajectories (every step locally compliant).** D2 claim `c` in unit `u` of D2 subject `s`. Specification `σ EVIDENCES u` and names module `m`; its evidentiary premises are `(u, σ, m)`. The solver reads its default parameters at run time from `defaults.toml`, which no specification names and no import analyzer can derive (the design's own incompleteness markers include “runtime configuration”). Under a reading in which “named” means declared only, a helper module imported by `m` serves equally.

1. **U1a — a candidate failure the pair cannot see.** An implementer iteration of item `I` edits `defaults.toml` (within its write scope) and the change degrades convergence. The designated runner executes `σ`'s configuration on the candidate. The independent reviewer, reading the convergence study, assesses it FAIL (`a-`); the iteration is returned.
   - In the admitted state, `starts` holds (the obligation is admitted) and `about(a-, s)` holds (admitted anchor, and the obligation-lineage term). `a-`'s recorded premises `(u, σ, m)` equal admitted content, so S3 derives **no rerun obligation**, and condition 4 makes a pair between `a-`'s content and admitted content **impossible**. Even if one were formed, `here` in the admitted state would read the reproduction itself, whose premises equal admitted content and whose outcome is the failing one, so it could not stand.
   - Admitted `s` is `P`; every validity-mode dependent of `s` is provisional; the D2 floor item is created **at once** (no rerun obligation exists). Only `a-`'s own assessor (a one-task execution that no longer exists), the owner's adjudication or `IMMATERIALITY`, each R3 here, lifts it.
   - Iteration 2 restores `defaults.toml`. In its frame `a-` still counts (own lineage), and no pair can form between iteration 1 and iteration 2 either (identical premises). The lineage cannot integrate its fixed candidate without a human record. Every other lineage whose frame requires `σ`'s obligation is blocked in the same way (§27.32's row says its pair disposes of it; no pair exists).
   - Abandoning item `I` changes nothing: the failure “counts in the admitted state at every later position whatever happens to the content”.
2. **U1b — a resolved failure resurrected by an unrelated candidate.** Earlier, `a-₀` failed `σ` on admitted `m0`; the fix `m1` integrated; a pair (reproduction on `m0`, trial on `m1`, mechanical oracle) disposed of `a-₀`, and it stands in the admitted state. Now any implementer iteration, of any item, edits `defaults.toml` so that `σ`'s configuration produces a slightly different output: better, and passing. The designated runner executes `σ` on that candidate as required evidence; the observation is admitted (never-lost). Its premises `(u, σ, m1)` equal the trial's, so it is an observation “on the trial's content” with another outcome. Condition 5 would fail, so the **withdrawal latch fires, globally and permanently**. `a-₀` counts again in the admitted state, where the content does not fail; “no pair can be formed again on that content”; S3 derives a rerun obligation that can never yield a disposition; when it closes, the D2 floor item latches. Whether the candidate is integrated or abandoned is irrelevant. The design's inference “another outcome on the same content, so nondeterministic in fact” is false here: the content differed, outside the premises.
3. **U1c — starvation.** While any frame's conformance run of `σ`'s configuration on premise-identical content is outstanding, “no pair is authorized”. In an active repository such runs overlap continuously, so pair authorization for a failure on a busy specification can be deferred indefinitely (restrictive, but it moves the floor item to its deadline path).

**What it violates.** The [evidence owner][evidence]: the target/execution separation and the applicability dimensions “subject/candidate” and “environment/backend/precision/configuration”: `a-` in U1a evaluated a candidate whose execution content differs from admitted content with certainty, so it is “a stale failing observation … not admissible evidence against current authority”, and the design's own pair, the cheap discriminating route it promises, is disabled exactly there. U1b presents a disagreement that discriminating evidence resolved as live (“explicit enough to distinguish resolved reassessment from still-live disagreement”), and attributes an outcome difference caused by changed content to nondeterminism without discriminating evidence. The stakeholder direction (“handle the important questions while letting the agents as well as the deterministic framework handle the rest automatically”) and §1.2 (“reduces, rather than transfers, complexity to agents and humans”) are contradicted: an ordinary returned implementer iteration on D1/D2-evidenced code becomes a mandatory human decision and a project-wide provisional status. The candidate's own guarantees quoted above are false on these trajectories. Two implementers can also read “on the trial's content” in condition 5 as premise identity (as `here` defines it) or as tree identity, with different withdrawal behaviour (C8); U1a holds under either reading.

**Earliest owner.** D3 §6.6 (the content comparator of the rerun trigger, condition 4, condition 5, the withdrawal latch, the custody guard and `here`), with §10 (what a realization records as executed content). Not Manual: §6.6 fixes the comparator explicitly (“every evidentiary premise it recorded”), and §22 and §27.32 fix the intended outcomes.

**Minimum repair direction.** Use one notion of “the same content” wherever a stop, a standing or a withdrawal is decided, and make it cover what the realization executed:

- record with each observation the identity of the tree it executed (already retained for the reproduction, §15.1), and decide conditions 1, 4 and 5, the withdrawal latch and the custody guard on execution identity: the premises plus all execution content of that tree, or the whole tree where the execution basis cannot be shown complete (the design's own incompleteness markers);
- derive the rerun obligation when that identity differs, so a candidate failure in un-premised content gets its scheduled pair (reproduction on the candidate tree, trial on the admitted tree; condition 2 and 3 still guard the cosmetic case);
- for `here`, either the same identity (with the cost of a rerun of every pair configuration after any execution-relevant change) or a negative-only form, “stands unless an admitted observation on `G`'s execution content reproduces the failing outcome”, which needs no positive rerun after unrelated changes and still catches a revert (§5);
- keep the named premises only as a scheduling optimization where a complete execution basis proves the rest unchanged.

**Required discriminators**, through real Admission, Derivation, discharge and inspection consumers:

- U1a with a runtime parameter file, rejected and abandoned: the scheduled pair on the admitted tree disposes of the failure, with no human item for a mechanical oracle; iteration 2 restoring the file integrates through the pair; another lineage is not blocked once the pair stands;
- U1b: a candidate that changes only un-premised content and alters the pair configuration's output does not withdraw the admitted pair;
- contrast (`6fe1000` A2 kept closed): an editorial frame change while the failure comes from admitted code still counts in the admitted state, and its trial on the admitted tree reproduces, so no pair forms;
- contrast: a differing observation on the trial's own execution identity still withdraws the pair.

### U2 — a never-admitted interpretive change (tighter oracle or tolerance, revised claim text) in an existing obligation qualifies the admitted subject with no possible pair

**Owner text at the candidate.**

- §10: specification = “evidence unit with `EVIDENCES` -> the unit holding the claim”. Obligation identity is therefore (specification unit, claim unit), independent of the claim's text or the specification's tolerance revision.
- §6.6, `about`: “s has a required evidence obligation in G in the lineage of the obligation a- names”; `starts`: “the obligation a- names, or one in its lineage, is required in the admitted state at some position ≤ n”.
- §6.6, start: “For an obligation already admitted, the ordinary case, that is at once”.
- §6.6, condition 2: “On every failing output, the trial's evaluated outcome differs from the reproduction's.” Rationale: “Condition 2 excludes an interpretive disagreement over one outcome, whatever premise moved”.
- §6.6, exception: “There is one exception. Where the specification revision `a-` applied was never admitted and the trial runs on admitted content: an output the trial lacks counts as different; the trial is read under its own admitted revision.”
- §6.6, dimension table: “interpretive premises. A change of them raises a reassessment obligation for the failing realization”, which “only `a-`'s assessor or the owner can turn into a disposition”.
- §6.6 and §0.1, C2b: “a claim added to an admitted subject in a frame never qualifies that subject outside the lineage, because the subject is only a frame anchor”; C2a: “a failing test-first specification in a rejected or abandoned iteration costs nothing outside its lineage”. §27.32: “Escape counting by never integrating the obligation | Nothing admitted requires it, so nothing admitted is qualified by it”.

**Trajectories (every step locally compliant).** D2 subject `s`, claim unit `u` (“the scheme's error is below 1e-5 on the benchmark”), specification `σ EVIDENCES u` with an interpretive oracle (an assessor reads the error table against the claim and the specification's tolerance). Admitted: passing assessment of `σ` on admitted content.

1. **U2a — a tightened tolerance.** An item asks for higher accuracy. The candidate tightens `σ`'s tolerance on the existing benchmark case to 1e-6 (the item's contract names `σ` in `evidence_specs`) and changes the solver, which does not reach it. The reviewer assesses the run FAIL on that case (`a-`). The item is later abandoned. `a-`'s obligation is admitted, so it counts in the admitted state at once and `about(a-, s)` holds. Its premises differ from admitted content (specification and code), so the rerun obligation runs the reproduction and a trial on admitted content. The solver's error on the case differs (the code changed), so condition 2 holds; condition 3 under the exception reads the trial under admitted `σ` (1e-5): PASS, and the reproduction under `σ'`: FAIL. The pair forms. **This variant holds.** Now the same candidate without the solver change (the tolerance tightened test-first, the code change still to come): the evaluated outcome on the failing case is identical on both contents, condition 2 fails, the case is not “an output the trial lacks”, and no pair can ever form.
2. **U2b — a revised claim in an existing unit.** A D2 proposal revises `u` to claim “below 1e-6” (or adds that sentence as a new claim inside `u`). The reviewer runs `σ` on the candidate and assesses FAIL against the revised claim; the proposal is rejected. The obligation `(σ, u)` is admitted, `s` is an admitted anchor and has the obligation, so `a-` counts in the admitted state at once. The governed-claim premise differs, so a reproduction and a trial run; the outputs do not depend on the claim's text, condition 2 fails, and no exception applies.

In both, admitted `s` (which contains neither the tightened tolerance nor the revised claim) is `P`; its dependents are provisional; the rerun obligation closes with no disposition and the D2 floor item latches; other lineages requiring `(σ, u)` are blocked; and only an owner adjudication or `IMMATERIALITY`, each R3 here, lifts it, after the candidate is long abandoned.

**What it violates.** The [evidence owner][evidence]: applicability over “evidence-spec revision … governed-claim revision … oracle semantics” differs with certainty, so `a-` is not admissible evidence against the admitted claim under the admitted specification revision; no competent admitted assessment conflicts with it in the owner's sense. The stakeholder direction and §1.2, as in U1. The candidate's own C2 rationale (“Until then nothing admitted contains the claim or specification it read”) and its C2b sentence: the subject is an admitted anchor and carries the obligation, so it is not “only a frame anchor”. `4ec0024` C2 is reproduced through the interpretive dimension: the start latch fires on obligation identity, which is coarser than the content `a-` read.

**Earliest owner.** D3 §6.6 (the never-admitted-revision exception and the start latch), with §10 (obligation granularity) and §22.

**Minimum repair direction.** Extend the existing exception from “an output the trial lacks” to every never-admitted interpretive premise `a-` applied: a specification revision that changed an existing check's oracle or tolerance, and a governed-claim revision. Where the trial runs on admitted content, condition 2 is then not required, the trial is read under the admitted specification and claim revisions, and condition 3 requires the mechanical oracle, or one independent assessor in one record, to give FAIL under `a-`'s revisions on the reproduction and PASS under the admitted revisions on the trial. This keeps `6fe1000` A2 closed (a failure from admitted code still fails under the admitted revisions) and `4ec0024` B′1 closed (editorially equal revisions cannot yield opposite verdicts from one honest reading over one outcome). The resulting pair already falls in the R2 case of §6.6 (reproduction on never-admitted content, trial on admitted content). Correct the C2b sentence and §27.32. The alternative is to state the cost (one owner judgment, R3 for D1/D2, per rejected interpretive proposal, with the admitted subject qualified meanwhile) with a §26 rate, and obtain the stakeholder's acceptance of it.

**Required discriminators:**

- U2a (tolerance tightened test-first, outcome unchanged) and U2b (claim revised and claim added inside an existing unit), each rejected and abandoned: admitted `s` unqualified after the pair, no human item;
- contrast: an editorial claim edit while the failure comes from admitted code still counts and forms no pair;
- contrast: the tightened tolerance or revised claim later integrated, after which the failing assessment counts and needs a disposition against admitted content.

## 2. Non-blocking findings

### N1 — historical governing-version adoption remains partly evidenced

Unchanged since `62c0b24` N1 (§0.4; stakeholder record, “Not covered”). The handoff independently binds this Review to 7.2.1, and the release state at the candidate names 7.2.1 accepted-current. Earliest owner: §0.4, pending stakeholder restatement.

### N2 — evaluated outcomes compared by byte identity: floating-point outputs and nondeterministic specifications

Conditions 1 and 5, the withdrawal latch and `here` compare evaluated outcomes by content identity. For numerical outputs whose low bits vary with execution attributes the specification leaves free (thread count, node, BLAS), pairs rarely form and are withdrawn by the next ordinary rerun on another node. §26 lists this as a rate trigger. The permissive side is not listed: `here` treats any bit difference as “another outcome”. After a pair, content `m0'` that reintroduces the failure with a cosmetic premise change, rerun on another node, gives a failing outcome with different bits; `here` holds and the pair stands there. A later passing assessment of that outcome by a lenient assessor makes the claim `T`. That is not a violation of the evidence owner (no conflicting assessment of that outcome is recorded, and `a-` is stale there), but §6.6's “never hides a failure that the current content reproduces” holds only for bit-identical reproduction. `here` is also undefined for a specification declared nondeterministic: the replicate rule decides conditions 1, 2 and 5, not `here`. Earliest owner: §6.6; Manual item 5. The negative-only form of `here` suggested under U1 removes the permissive side.

### N3 — the second R2 pair exception does not establish its rationale

§6.6 makes R2 “an assessor-read pair whose reproduction ran on content that was never admitted and whose trial ran on admitted content”, because it “shows only that a candidate's failure was the candidate's own”. A frame that changes only a comment while the failure comes from admitted D2 code gives such a pair once admitted content is later fixed: the reproduction ran on never-admitted content (the comment), the trial on the fixed admitted content, and the outcome flips. The failure belonged to admitted content, yet the disposition is R2. If the fix lands before the first rerun obligation closes, the deferred floor item is never created either. The evidence owner is satisfied (the pair is discriminating evidence), so this is a D3-internal inconsistency in human routing. Repair: require that the trial ran on the admitted content at `a-`'s base position. Earliest owner: §6.6 decision class; §7.9.

### N4 — pair authorization and floor deferral read facts the ledger does not record

“No pair is authorized while an execution of its configuration on either content is outstanding” is decided at Admission from the outstanding-envelope set. Task issue is operator-journal state (§6.3), so the decision is neither recorded nor replayable from the ledger (P1, C3). The floor's “passed its deadline” needs time to enter as a recorded typed fact (C3), and the text names no such record. Nor does it say whether a rerun obligation re-derived after each content change keeps its first deadline. Policy sets the deadline unbounded, although floors are “fixed by system semantics; policy may raise them, never lower them”. After a withdrawal, S3 still derives a rerun obligation for content on which no pair can form, which only delays the floor. Earliest owner: §6.6, §7.9; Manual items 5 and 14.

### N5 — the never-lost resubmission after a refused envelope is discretionary

Step 3 now retains the refused record's bytes and hands them to the re-issued task, “which may resubmit them under its own envelope”. A re-issued reviewer that reaches PASS can decline, and the failing assessment then exists only as inert bytes that S0 never reads. That contradicts “nothing reported or observed adversely is lost”. Make the resubmission mechanical (R0), or route the retained record to the principal as a report. The case is rare (retirement after host loss with an incomplete journal). `4ec0024` N8 is partly closed. Earliest owner: §6.8 step 3.

### N6 — “admitted” for the latch and for the exception

The latch fires when the obligation is required in the admitted state “at some position”, including through an absorption. The exception asks whether the specification revision “was never admitted”. Whether absorbed content counts as admitted is not stated. A transient foreign push of a rejected candidate, reverted at once, latches its failures permanently. If absorption counts as admission, the exception then no longer applies to them, and they go to the owner. Define the term once. Earliest owner: §6.6 (editorial).

### N7 — residual cost not stated

- A lineage that drops its own failing test-first case in the next iteration cannot form a pair (the trial is on its own frame, outside the exception) and needs an owner disposition (R3 for D1/D2). That routing is defensible, since the implementer removed a check it failed, but §0.1's cost statement and §26 omit it.
- A failing assessment that is later revoked or superseded still withdraws a pair permanently (existence-keyed). That is restrictive and costs one more owner disposition.

Earliest owner: §0.1, §26.
