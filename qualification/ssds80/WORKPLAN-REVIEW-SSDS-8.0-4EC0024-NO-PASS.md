## SERIOUS CHALLENGE

**None against accepted governing authority.** The [kernel][kernel] says: “A coherent parent with a wrong child is an ordinary blocker.” The governing [evidence owner][evidence] is coherent and realizable here:

- “A valid failing observation is evidence. A stale failing observation is not admissible evidence against current authority. A stale passing observation is not admissible confirmation.”
- “Use `review-required` when applicability is uncertain.”
- “When competent evidence assessments materially conflict, preserve the disagreement and route adjudication to the governing claim/owner or obtain discriminating evidence. Do not resolve material assessment disagreement by majority vote, latest-editor order, reviewer seniority, count, temperature, or serialized record position. When a durable representation stores multiple assessments, supersession/adjudication must be explicit enough to distinguish resolved reassessment from still-live disagreement. An unresolved material disagreement keeps dependent present guidance/closure visibly qualified.”

The family rule revision 18 adopts (“machinery may only make a failing assessment start counting and schedule its disposition; only a judgment that addresses it, or discriminating evidence, may stop it”) is a sound concretization of that owner. All three blockers are places where the candidate's definitions do not meet it: B′ calls a pair discriminating when nothing in it discriminates; C1 lets a stop that discriminating evidence earned lapse mechanically, so a resolved disagreement is presented as live; C2 starts counting where no competent assessments conflict. They lie in D3.

## Verdict

**NO-PASS.** Three blockers remain, all in the mechanisms revision 18 added or kept while applying the family rule:

- **B′ — the discriminating pair does not require the evidence to discriminate.** Conditions 1-4 of §6.6 compare the reproduction with the failing observation, require that some premise changed, and require the trial to be *assessed* passing. No condition compares the trial's observation with the failing one, so the passing side of an interpretive disagreement wins after a cosmetic premise change (the variant `6fe1000` B1 named explicitly). Condition 4 reads only *assessments* on the trial side, so unassessed failing trial executions of a nondeterministic failure are ignored (`6fe1000` B2 by another route).
- **C1 — a disposition earned by discriminating evidence lapses mechanically.** A pair-backed supersession stands only at its trial assessment's currency. At every later change of what the trial evaluated, a long-fixed failure becomes adverse again, the claim is labelled `disputed`, and a new pair and a new independent assessment are owed (R3, a human, for D1/D2). Failing assessments therefore accumulate without end and resurrect at each change.
- **C2 — the admitted state is qualified by failing assessments on obligations and claims it never contained.** Because every failing assessment counts in every frame from admission, and a frame record's anchors include the admitted subjects that hold its frame's target, a failing assessment of a new test or a new claim in an unintegrated candidate makes the admitted subject `P`. No pair can ever form for it, since the specification or the claim does not exist on admitted content. Only an owner's `IMMATERIALITY` judgment (R3 for D1/D2) lifts it, even after the candidate is abandoned.

Revision 18 closes `6fe1000` A1-A3, the unchanged-content member of B1, and the reproduction-side member of B2. It closes N2, N3, N4, N5 and N7, and closes N6, N8 and N9 in part. The “stop counting” family has recurred again, now as “how long a stop stands” (C1) and “where counting starts” (C2), after the simplification the convergence owner mandated. Per the [convergence owner][convergence] (“post-simplification recurrence, or evidence governing abstraction is wrong -> bounded reconsideration of the earliest affected abstraction”), this record routes it as one family with three members. The repair directions below are three narrow rule changes, not new mechanisms.

Requested checks: the four routing tests pass, and `git diff --check` is clean. The model check is green at depths 4 and 5: 27/27 discriminators, with 99,814 and 2,944,835 transitions, matching §0.1. It is red under `REV17=1`, `REV16=1`, `REV14=1`, `REV13=1`, `REV12=1`, `REV11=1` and `REV10=1`. Every sensitivity mode fails P9, and `REV16`-`REV10` also fail P7 and Q. `REV17` fails only P9 and the oracle. The model represents “discriminating” as a trusted boolean (`disc`), which both its predicates and its oracle accept, so B′ is unrepresentable in it. It cannot see C2, because it has no frames. It does reproduce C1, as an expected value: its own semantics give `P` after a pair-backed supersession followed by an edit and a passing rerun (§7). None of these checks establishes semantic adequacy.

## 1. Blockers by earliest owner

### B′ — the pair's conditions let an interpretive disagreement and a nondeterministic failure be settled without discrimination

**Owner text at the candidate.**

- §6.6, Discriminating pair: the designated runner executes “the **reproduction**: `a-`'s configuration on the content `a-` evaluated” and “the **trial**: the same configuration on the evaluating frame's content”. “An assessment of the trial by a qualified assessor independent of the claim's authors may name `a-` in `supersedes`. Admission authorizes it mechanically only when the pair **discriminates**:
  1. the reproduction's observation is identical, by content identity, to the observation `a-` assessed;
  2. the trial is assessed passing;
  3. the content evaluated by the reproduction and by the trial differs in at least one recorded evidentiary premise;
  4. no admitted execution of the configuration on `a-`'s content has produced a different observation, and no admitted assessment of an execution of it on the trial's content fails.”
- §6.6, same paragraph: “The pair is discriminating evidence in the evidence owner's sense: the failure reproduces on the old content and not on the new. A rerun on unchanged content can never form a pair, so an interpretive disagreement over one observation is settled only by `a-`'s own assessor, the owner's adjudication or `IMMATERIALITY`”. Also: “Every designated-runner execution is admitted as an observation (§10 custody), so no rerun can be hidden.”
- §6.6 dimension table: evidentiary premises are “governed-claim content; execution content the specification named”, “recorded at admission as content identities”.
- §6.8 step 3: a record may name `x` in `supersedes` “only as `x`'s own assessor's reassessment, the owner's adjudication, or the assessment of a trial in a discriminating pair that Admission verifies”.
- §27.30, attacks: “Make a cosmetic change, then form a pair | Condition 1 needs the identical failing observation on the old content and condition 2 a pass on the new; a cosmetic change that genuinely flips a deterministic outcome is a real change”. §22 lists the cosmetic case only “where the reproduction is not identical or the trial's content changes no evaluated premise”.

**Trajectories (every step locally compliant).** D3 claim `c` in subject `s`. A deterministic specification `σ EVIDENCES c` and names module `m`. Assessor `X` assesses a realization PASS (`a+`). Assessor `Y`, a fresh-context review execution, assesses the same observation `O` FAIL (`a_Y`); they read the tolerance differently.

1. **B′1 — interpretive disagreement after a cosmetic change.** An implementer task changes a comment in `m` (or reformats it) and the change integrates. Integration is not blocked: `c` was already `P` and step 8 forbids only collateral regression. `m`'s content identity now differs from `a_Y`'s recorded premise, so S3 derives the rerun obligation. The reproduction on `a_Y`'s content yields `O` again, byte-identical (condition 1). The trial on the new content yields the same numbers. Condition 3 holds, since `m`'s identity changed. `X`, qualified and not an author of `c`, assesses the trial PASS under the same reading as before and names `a_Y` in `supersedes` (condition 2). No other execution or assessment exists (condition 4). Admission authorizes. `disp(a_Y) = T`, the `disputed` label and the adjudication obligation go away, and `ev(s, e) = T`. Nothing discriminated: the observation the trial produced is the observation `Y` failed. The opposing side won by being later after a cosmetic edit, which is “latest-editor order … or serialized record position”. `Y` could only reverse this by assessing the trial FAIL *before* `X`'s record is admitted. That makes the outcome depend on record order, and `Y` is a one-task execution that no longer exists (§9.6). The same works inside a frame: `a_Y`'s premises include an editorially changed claim, the trial runs on admitted content, and `X` supersedes.
2. **B′2 — trial-side nondeterminism.** `σ` exercises a race; it is not declared nondeterministic. `a_Y` records a genuine failure. After any premise change, the reproduction happens to reproduce `O` identically, and no other execution on the old content exists. Trials on the new content fail and pass intermittently. Every designated-runner execution is admitted as an observation, including step 7-8 reruns from unrelated integrations. Only a passing one is assessed, by an independent assessor who names `a_Y`. Condition 4's trial side reads “admitted assessment … fails”, not observations, so the unassessed failing trial observations are invisible to it, and the pair is authorized. The old-content side is checked against observations and the new-content side against assessments; nothing explains the asymmetry. Condition 4 is also evaluated only at admission (`superseded` is S0 syntax), so a later admitted execution on `a_Y`'s content that differs never withdraws the disposition.

**What it violates.** The [evidence owner][evidence]: “route adjudication to the governing claim/owner or obtain discriminating evidence. Do not resolve material assessment disagreement by … latest-editor order … or serialized record position”; “Do not suppress contradictory admissible observations to manufacture a pass”; “An assembled outcome after several simultaneous changes cannot be attributed to one mechanism without discriminating evidence.” The candidate's own sentences: “the failure reproduces on the old content and not on the new”, which condition 2 checks only by an assessor's reading, and “an interpretive disagreement over one observation is settled only by `a-`'s own assessor, the owner's adjudication or `IMMATERIALITY`”, which B′1 contradicts. §27.30's attack row is false as stated: condition 2 is an assessment, not an observed outcome flip.

**Earliest owner.** D3 §6.6 (pair conditions) and §6.8 step 3 (authorization). Not Manual: the conditions are stated exactly at D3, and §22 and §27.30 fix the intended outcome.

**Minimum repair direction.** Make the discrimination lie in the observations under one reading:

- (i) the trial's observation differs from the reproduction's in the outcome the specification's oracle evaluates, not merely in byte identity (log lines, line numbers or timestamps must not qualify);
- (ii) the superseding assessor assesses the reproduction failing and the trial passing, so that one reading yields opposite verdicts on the two contents. Where the oracle is mechanical, its verdicts on the two runs suffice. An interpretive disagreement over identical outcomes then cannot form a pair;
- (iii) condition 4's trial side reads every admitted *observation* of the configuration on the trial's content, not only assessments;
- (iv) a later admitted execution on `a-`'s content with a different observation, or a later failing assessment on the trial's content, withdraws the pair's standing. That is a monotone start-latch, allowed by the family rule.

Define at D3 which part of an observation “identical” compares (N3).

**Required discriminators**, through real Admission, Derivation, discharge and inspection consumers:

- B′1 with a comment-only change, with integrated content and in a frame: the claim stays qualified, labelled, with the adjudication obligation open;
- B′2 with a failing, unassessed trial observation admitted before the passing trial: stays qualified;
- B′2 with a differing old-content execution admitted *after* the pair: qualification returns;
- contrast: a genuine fix whose trial outcome flips, assessed failing-then-passing by one independent assessor, with no contradicting observation, lifts the qualification.

The model needs an observation-outcome dimension per execution, and an oracle that does not trust `disc`.

### C1 — a stop earned by discriminating evidence lapses at every later change, so failures resurrect and accumulate

**Owner text at the candidate.**

- §6.6, Effective disposition: “`eff(d) = … current(d)` otherwise, `d` support-tier (a superseder, IMMATERIALITY)”; “A support-tier disposition stands at its own currency. Its basis must still hold in the evaluating frame”.
- §6.6, What does not end a disagreement: “A disposition whose basis has moved”. Re-disposal: “After a disposition stops standing, the owner disposes of the failing assessment again … a further supersession of the failing assessment … or a supersession of the stale superseder”.
- §6.6: `adverse(s, e) = { failing assessments a- admissible for e with about(a-, s) }`. Supersession of `a-` ends its currency, not its adversity.
- §6.6: “When the recorded evidentiary premises of an adverse failing assessment `a-` differ from the evaluating frame's content, S3 derives a rerun obligation”. This is not conditioned on the absence of a standing disposition.
- §6.6 decision class: supersessions that dispose of another actor's failing assessment are R2, and R3 “where any subject the failing assessment is about is, or has been, D1 or D2 authority”. §7.5: R3 is “authenticated human”; §7.9: “any decision R3 owns” is `CRITICAL`.
- §6.8 step 3: a stale superseder `d` may itself be superseded only “as `d`'s own assessor's reassessment, the owner's adjudication, or the assessment of a trial in a discriminating pair”. The pair is defined only against a failing `a-`.
- §22 requires the lapse: “a superseder whose basis moved” must leave the claim qualified. §0.1 and §6.6: “An ordinary fix thus costs two reruns and one assessment”.

**Trajectory (every step locally compliant).** Claim `c`, specification `σ` naming `m`. `a_Y` fails on content `m0`. A genuine fix `m1` is made. The pair forms: identical reproduction on `m0`, passing trial on `m1`, independent assessment `d1` superseding `a_Y`. The claim becomes `T`. This is the intended path, and it holds.

Months later an unrelated refactor produces `m2`. `d1`'s execution basis is no longer current, so `eff(d1) = F`, `disp(a_Y) = F` and `undisputed = P`. The ordinary passing rerun on `m2` does not help: it is not authorized to supersede `d1` or `a_Y` unless it forms a new pair. `c` is again labelled `disputed`, with an adjudication obligation and an S3 rerun obligation. Re-disposal needs a new reproduction of `m0` (re-executing a retained old tree, possibly an HPC campaign) and a new independent assessment naming `a_Y`. For a D1/D2 claim that assessment is an R3 human decision.

Every failing assessment ever admitted on `c` does the same at every later change of what its trial evaluated. Since revision 18 counts every frame iteration's failing assessment from admission, that set grows with the project's whole development history. The candidate's own model gives this result: the ledger `PASS, FAIL, MOVE, PASS supersedes=FAIL (disc)` evaluates to `T`, and appending `MOVE, PASS` evaluates to `P` (§7).

**What it violates.** The [evidence owner][evidence]: “supersession/adjudication must be explicit enough to distinguish resolved reassessment from still-live disagreement”; “A stale failing observation is not admissible evidence against current authority”. After a verified pair, the disagreement *was* resolved by discriminating evidence, and resurrecting it presents a resolved disagreement as live, using an observation of `m0` against current authority at `m2`. Also: “Stop recursive evidence inflation when the material claim and its evidence-route integrity are sufficiently established.” Nothing is lost by letting the pair stand. Stale passing evidence never confirms, so `c` at `m2` still needs a current passing assessment, and a reintroduced failure is a new failing assessment. The cost statement in §0.1 and §6.6 is false: an ordinary fix costs two reruns and one assessment *per later change, for ever*. §26's reopen trigger (“disagreement becomes routine”) is made to fire by design, and §1.2 (“reduces, rather than transfers, complexity to agents and humans”) and the stakeholder's direction (the human handles “the important questions”) are contradicted for D1/D2 claims.

**Earliest owner.** D3 §6.6 (`eff` of a pair-backed superseder; the rerun-obligation trigger), with §22.

**Minimum repair direction.** Let a supersession authorized by a verified pair stand on the pair's immutable facts and the trial assessment's own standing: not superseded, not revoked, not under an open blocking challenge, not withheld by `cont`, and not withdrawn by B′ (iv). It should not depend on the currency of the trial's execution basis, because the pair disposes of `a-`; it does not support `c`. Keep basis-currency standing for `IMMATERIALITY` judgments whose prerequisite may move (`97e753f` B1 stays closed). Derive the rerun obligation only for adverse failing assessments with no standing disposition.

**Required discriminators:**

- a pair disposition, then an unrelated edit and a passing rerun: unqualified, no `disputed` label, no new obligation;
- contrast: an edit that reintroduces the failure yields a new failing assessment and the claim is `P`;
- contrast: `IMMATERIALITY` whose prerequisite moved still lapses.

### C2 — failing assessments on obligations and claims the admitted state never contained qualify admitted subjects, with no mechanical disposition

**Owner text at the candidate.**

- §6.6: “**A failing assessment counts in every frame from its admission**, the admitted frame included, whatever frame it was made in and whatever becomes of that frame”; “A failing assessment for an obligation that exists only in a frame counts in the admitted state under the same rule; the owner disposes of it with `IMMATERIALITY` if the obligation was never admitted.”
- §6.6, anchors: “every subject whose scope contained the evaluated target at admission, in the admitted state at its base and, for a frame record, in its frame”. Also: `qual(s) = ⋀_{e named by an admitted failing assessment a- with about(a-, s)} undisputed(s, e) -- whether or not e is still required`, and `undisputed(s, e) = P` whenever `adverse(s, e)` holds an undisposed member, whether or not any passing assessment for `e` exists.
- §6.6 dimension table: “protocol obligation | … Assessments for different obligations never conflict.”
- §6.6, pair: the configuration includes the “specification unit”, and the trial runs on “the evaluating frame's content”.
- §27.30: “Flood frames with failing assessments to make admitted claims provisional | … each is answered by a scheduled pair”.

**Trajectories (every step locally compliant).**

1. **C2a — a new test.** An implementer writes a new specification `σ'` for existing claim `c` in D2 subject `s`, test first. The reviewer assesses the candidate's designated-runner execution FAIL (`a'`), and the iteration continues. `a'` names obligation `e'`, which exists only in the frame. `s` is an anchor, so `about(a', s)` holds in the admitted state, `qual(s) = P` and `valid(s) ≤ P`. Every dependent of `s` in validity mode becomes provisional, and discharge across the project waits on it.

   No pair can form on admitted content, because `σ'` is not there. The only lift is an owner `IMMATERIALITY` judgment, which is R3 (a human) because `s` is D2. If the candidate is abandoned, `s` stays `P` until that judgment. The admitted state holds no assessment of `e'` at all, passing or failing, under any accepted specification, so there is nothing for any assessment to conflict with.
2. **C2b — a new claim.** The frame adds claim `c_new` to admitted subject `s`, with its specification. Its failing assessment anchors `s` (the frame's `s` contains the target), so admitted `s` is `P` for a claim it does not contain. Again no pair can form, and only an owner judgment lifts it.
3. **C2c — cross-lineage spread.** Implementers A and B work on different items touching `s`. B's rejected iteration's failing assessment counts in A's frame too. A's integration cannot discharge until someone runs B's pair against A's content and an independent assessor (R3 for D1/D2) supersedes B's failure there. C2a and C2b reach every concurrent frame in the same way.

**What it violates.** The [evidence owner][evidence]: assessment disagreement is defined “when competent evidence assessments materially conflict”. In C2a and C2b no admitted assessment of the obligation exists to conflict with, and the observation concerns a specification or claim that current authority does not contain: “A stale failing observation is not admissible evidence against current authority.” Applicability must be “recoverable over evidence-spec revision, subject/candidate, … and protocol obligation”, and both those dimensions are certain here, not uncertain, so `review-required` is not the owner's prescribed status. The candidate's own table says “Assessments for different obligations never conflict”, and §27.30 says every flood is “answered by a scheduled pair”, which C2a-C2b refute.

The stakeholder direction also applies: the human should handle “the important questions while letting the agents as well as the deterministic framework handle the rest automatically and seamlessly”. Test-first iteration on a D1/D2 claim now routes a human R3 judgment per rejected iteration. In revision 17 such failures did not reach the admitted state until their lineage integrated. The count-from-admission repair over-reached on the *start* side for content and obligations that are not admitted anywhere.

**Earliest owner.** D3 §6.6 (frame dimension and anchors for frame records), with §7.5 and §7.9 for class and floor.

**Minimum repair direction.** Keep “machinery may only start counting”, but start counting in the admitted state, and in other lineages' frames, by a monotone latch on an admitted fact: the failing assessment's obligation is, or has been, admitted at some position, by any route (absorption, another lineage, a new task obligation). Until then it counts in every frame of its own lineage and blocks that candidate's integration. This keeps `6fe1000` A1-A3 closed:

- A1: the specification and obligation were admitted already;
- A2: likewise;
- A3: no `Δ(X)` is read.

Anchor a frame record in the admitted state only on admitted subjects that held its target *at its base* (C2b). For C2c, either accept the spread with an explicit cost statement and a §26 rate, or derive the pair obligation per frame and state its decision class.

**Required discriminators:**

- C2a and C2b, rejected and abandoned: admitted `s` unqualified, no human item;
- contrast: the same specification later integrated, or its content absorbed, with the earlier failing assessment then counting;
- C2c: B's failure on an obligation not yet admitted does not block A.

## 2. Non-blocking findings

### N1 — historical governing-version adoption remains partly evidenced

Unchanged since `62c0b24` N1 (§0.4; stakeholder record, “Not covered”). The handoff independently binds this Review to 7.2.1, and the release state at the candidate names 7.2.1 accepted-current. Earliest owner: §0.4, pending stakeholder restatement.

### N2 — D1/D2 dispositions of candidate failures are human work per iteration

Even after C2's repair, every rejected iteration's failing assessment on an admitted D1/D2 obligation counts in the admitted state. The pair that disposes of it is an R3 human assessment, and the disagreement floor raises latched human attention. Merged inspector entries (§9.8) let one human judgment answer many floor items, but each disposing assessment is still a human record. This is burden transfer against §1.2 and SD-2, and it is visible only through the §26 trigger. Options: classify as R2 a pair whose trial runs on admitted content and restores the pre-existing admitted status, with the floor item as human visibility; or state the per-iteration human cost and get stakeholder acceptance. Earliest owner: §6.6 decision class; §7.5.

### N3 — “identical, by content identity” is not defined on the part of the observation compared

An observation is “the realization record's outcome plus references to native artifacts (logs, data) by content identity” (§10). If logs are compared, timestamps, host names and path-dependent tracebacks make condition 1 almost never hold, and every failure goes to the owner. The same happens for numerical code whose retained old tree runs in a changed environment that the specification does not fix. If only the outcome is compared, B′1 is easier still. This is the `6fe1000` A3 pattern in the pair: two implementers can build different conditions 1 and 4 from the same text (C8). Earliest owner: §6.6; Manual item 11 for formats.

### N4 — “every designated-runner execution is admitted” has no stated mechanism

§6.6 cites “§10 custody” for “no rerun can be hidden”. §10 does not say it. §7.5 records R0 “evidence and check executions” as “derived state or operator journal”, which is private, non-canonical state (§15.1). Policy-designated external custody runners (§10) could report one execution of several. Condition 4's first half and §27.30's attack row rest on this sentence. State the mechanism (for example, the Dispatcher submits every execution it starts, and custody records enumerate runs), or bound the claim. Earliest owner: §6.6, §10.

### N5 — a failing trial cannot satisfy condition 2, yet “supersedes `a-` as well”

§6.6 says “A failing trial supersedes `a-` as well, by the same authorization”, and §22 says “A failing trial supersedes it”. Condition 2 requires the trial to be assessed passing. An implementer must choose. Either choice is restrictive, because the failing trial assessment stays adverse. Earliest owner: §6.6 (editorial).

### N6 — step 3 keeps a contradictory tail; model and workplan differ on unauthorized superseders

Step 3 reads “a record whose `supersedes` is not so authorized is rejected and its refusal recorded, never admitted; and is otherwise admitted as a non-independent judgment that cannot satisfy one”. Whether a superseder that fails only the “independent of the claim's authors” clause is rejected, or admitted with `superseded(x)` true and `eff = F`, is ambiguous. In the second reading the old denial of service survives: a claim's author can de-currency a passing assessment it is entitled to supersede as its assessor. The model admits an unauthorized superseder “as an ordinary passing assessment”, while the workplan rejects the record. Earliest owner: §6.8 step 3 (editorial). `6fe1000` N6 is otherwise closed.

### N7 — residual wording

- §7.9's disagreement floor says “is D1 or D2 authority”, where §6.6 says “is, or has been”.
- §7.4's `qual` line still reads “an obligation that has stopped being required”, although `qual` now covers obligations never admitted.
- S4's new clause is tagged `[6.6]`, though it is a D3 concretization of the owner, not 6.6 text.
- The model's docstring still says “must report failures on P1-P6 or Q”, which `REV17` does not do.
- A model discriminator is still named “configuration rerun passes and supersedes”, with `disc` defaulting to `True`.

Earliest owner: those sections and the model.

### N8 — unlisted retired-key envelopes lose the never-lost record's original bytes

Step 3 now names the list's source and raises a re-issue obligation for a refused never-lost record. A re-issued task is a fresh execution, which may not re-assess as the lost one did.
