## Blockers

### B1 — Re-identification plus any amendment still escapes a standing review veto and both brief carries (family: reach, carry and resolution keyed on plan-item identity; recurrence of 5895687 B1)

**Text.** §5.10 (b), transformation `REVIEW` row (and `CONFORMANCE`, "as `REVIEW` of a transformation"): reach is "every transformation with the same changed-unit content …, under the same plan item at any of its revisions or under an item revision, in any item or plan, with the same prose and contract (item identity excluded, SD-33)". §5.10 (a): "Every reach contains the target; reaching further is what keeps amendment, rebase, re-dispatch, a new run or a composition of judgments from escaping an objection." §0.3 R9: "an amendment of the item revision with unchanged candidate content is reached"; "A review or acceptance disagreement is never resolved by re-dispatch, count or order." §13.4: item prose is "every byte of the item's part outside its `ssds-item` block"; §1.3: a `heading` term is "the span from the ATX heading path `A > B` to the next heading", so the heading line is item prose; §11.1: an item is "a section whose heading path the manifest declares as a part"; §11.4: "item topology (`depends_on`, added or removed items) … always need whole-plan review". §16.5: the review carry selects "every standing transformation `NO_PASS` or `NONCONFORMANT` whose reach includes the candidate"; the re-review carry supplies "the earlier iterations' review findings that no later judgment resolved (a finding is resolved when a later review of the same item records it `RESOLVED` …)"; the plan carry selects every standing rejection "that names any item the acceptance asks about, at any revision of that item …, or that names an item revision, in any item or plan (removed items included), with the same prose and fixed fields as a revision of an item asked about", and it "stays carried until a later plan acceptance of the item … records it `RESOLVED`". §32.2: "An amendment of a plan item's prose or fixed fields is a new target a standing rejection does not reach; the rejection is carried into the next acceptor's brief (§16.5)". §31.6 (X): "an amendment of `A`'s contract with the same changed-unit content stays vetoed as before; contrast: an item whose prose or contract differs is not reached".

**Trajectory (a): transformation review, conforming at every step.**

1. Plan `P`, item `A` (part `P.A`, heading `Solver timestep`, `item = "solver-timestep"`), revision `a1`, accepted. `A`'s task produces `X1` changing unit `u` to content `C`. An authenticated independent human `H` records `REVIEW NO_PASS (X1)`. It is never-lost and stands.
2. The plan's principal edits the plan: part `P.A` is removed and part `P.A2` declared, heading `Solver time step` (one byte added), `item = "solver-time-step"`, the rest of the prose and the whole contract byte-identical to `a1`'s. This is a topology change, so whole-plan review is derived (§11.4); there is no plan rejection, so the plan-acceptance brief carries nothing about `X1`, and a fresh independent acceptor records `ACCEPTED (o, {A2: a2})` on what reads as the same item. Nothing in §8.3 refuses the edit: a `NO_PASS` is not a blocking challenge, so the step 4 freeze does not apply.
3. The Dispatcher issues `A2`'s task; it produces `X2` with the same changed-unit content `C` (for instance by re-applying `X1`'s diff).
4. Reach of `H`'s `NO_PASS` at `X2`: "the same plan item at any of its revisions" fails (`A2` is not `A`); "an item revision … with the same prose and contract" fails (the heading byte). So `X2` is not reached. The review carry selects by that reach, so `H`'s judgment is not supplied; the re-review carry is per iteration of an obligation, and `X2` is the first iteration of `A2`'s.
5. A fresh independent reviewer records `PASS (X2)`, unvetoed; `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` is not derived; `X2` integrates content `C` that a standing, unsuperseded, unrelieved independent `NO_PASS` denies, after re-dispatch under a new identity, with the owner never routed and no later judge having seen the objection.

Had step 2 renamed the heading of `A` without re-identifying it, `X2` would be reached ("the same plan item at any of its revisions") and the `PASS` vetoed. The two edits change exactly the same judged bytes (the heading line); only the part id and `item` key differ, so item identity still decides the veto. The same holds with any other prose byte, or with one contract field tightened (R1 for the same item, a different contract for a re-identified one), and with a copy of `A` into another plan carrying any such change.

**Trajectory (b): plan carry, the sibling.** `H` records `PLAN_ACCEPTANCE REJECTED (o, {A: a1})`. The principal removes `A` and adds `A2` as in (a)2. The veto does not reach `A2`'s acceptance; that is the disclosed amendment residual (§32.2), whose stated safeguard is the carry. The whole-plan brief asks about `A2` and the other items; the rejection names `A`, which is not asked about, and no revision of an asked item has `a1`'s prose and fixed fields, so it is not carried. A fresh acceptor records `ACCEPTED (o, {A2: a2})` without having seen it. With the same edit made to `A` in place, the rejection is carried ("names any item the acceptance asks about, at any revision of that item"). §32.2's promise ("carried into the next acceptor's brief") is not delivered on this input; this is the d6734c4 B1 shape (a selector that misses an amendment route), reached here through identity.

**Siblings (same cause, checked).** (i) Review/conformance reach, same-item disjunct: fails as (a). (ii) Review carry: follows the reach, fails as (a). (iii) Re-review carry: "earlier iterations" and "a later review of the same item" are per item and obligation, so re-identification with a content change drops every unresolved earlier finding that the same iteration under the old identity would have carried (permissive loss of disclosure); and a `RESOLVED` recorded by the review of the re-identified item never resolves them (restrictive). (iv) Plan carry: fails as (b). (v) Plan carry ending, "a later plan acceptance of the item": after a byte-identical re-identification, the rejecter's or the owner's relieving acceptance of `A2` is not "of the item" `A`, so a resolved rejection stays carried into every later brief that matches by prose and fixed fields, in any plan (restrictive). Held: gate `REJECT`, `ACCEPTANCE FAIL` (keyed on the subject triple), `EQUIVALENCE`, `COMPLETENESS` (keyed on unit revisions and scopes), the byte-identical re-identification and copy of 5895687 B1 (closed by SD-33's realization), objective-only and multi-item plan edits. I stopped there: further identity routes (unit `renamed_from`, splits of an item into two) are the same cause, and their enumeration would be implementation work.

**Why this is a defect, not a disclosed trade-off.** The Manual states as the purpose of reach that amendment and re-dispatch do not escape an objection (§5.10 (a), R9), and keeps "the same plan item at any of its revisions" precisely so that an amendment does not escape (closure map §5, author's concretization under item 19). That protection is conditional on the part id and `item` key, which a locally compliant plan edit changes without any judgment of the objection. §32.2 discloses neither the review escape nor the carry gap; it discloses only that byte-identical items share reach. §31.6 (X) would pass an implementation with this defect, since its contrast case ("an item whose prose or contract differs is not reached") asserts it.

Workflow owner (`workflow-and-workplans.md`, line 59): "A current handoff must be snapshot-complete for its **governed task scope**: recover … unresolved blockers/Challenge … without hidden chat or unavailable history"; line 104: "A synthesizer reconciles findings through evidence and owner adjudication; it cannot erase unresolved contradictory material evidence by vote." Manual §0.4 A4 (a duty a task owes is carried in its brief or enforced mechanically), R9 and §5.10 (a) as quoted; SD-33 ("key plan and transformation-review reach and both brief carries on what was judged rather than on plan-item identity"). Workplan §21 items 5 (judgment derivation) and 10 (brief templates).

**Earliest owner.** Manual §5.10 (b) transformation `REVIEW`/`CONFORMANCE` row and §16.5 (review, re-review and plan carries and the plan carry's ending); the choice between the options below belongs to the stakeholder, because SD-33's verbatim key and R9 cannot both be met by an item-revision key. §31.6 (X), §32.2 and the closure map follow.

**Family assessment (convergence owner, "Review strategy after recurrence").** One cause, the same as 5895687 B1: a reach, carry or resolution condition names what was judged through a mutable identity (the plan part and `item` key). SD-33's realization re-keyed only the case where nothing judged changed; wherever the rule must also survive a change of what was judged (amendment for reviews; amendment for the plan carry; later iterations for the re-review carry), it still uses identity. It is a non-local recurrence in the sense that the repair added a key (prose and contract) beside the old one rather than replacing it, so the escape moved to the combination. The minimum repair directions below remove identity rather than adding a further disjunct.

**Repair options (for the stakeholder).**

- **(1) Recommended, subtractive: key reviews on content alone, and the plan carry on the plan subject.** Review/conformance reach: every transformation with the same changed-unit content, under any item, revision or plan (the 5895687 Review's parenthetical alternative). It deletes both item conditions, so neither amendment nor re-identification changes it; the review carry follows. Re-review carry: the unresolved findings of earlier reviews of transformations on the same changed units, or simply on the same plan subject, removed items included, resolved by any later independent review recording them `RESOLVED`. Plan carry: every standing rejection naming any item of the plan subject being accepted, removed items included, plus the existing cross-plan prose-and-fixed-fields key (re-identification within a plan always needs whole-plan review, §11.4, so this catches it). Costs: a `NO_PASS` on content `C` under one item vetoes a `PASS` of `C` under an unrelated item until its author or the owner relieves that target (one record); a copy into another plan with an amendment is neither reached nor carried, and §32.2 must say so.
- **(2) Disclosure only.** Keep the reach; state in §32.2 that re-identifying an item together with any change of its prose or contract is a new target for the review veto and that both carries then rely on the inspector's history; drop (X)'s implication that identity-keyed escape is intended. The veto stays escapable by a plan edit.
- **(3) Item lineage (adds mechanism).** Make a re-identified part declare lineage (`renamed_from`) and refuse an R2 topology edit that removes a part carrying a standing objection without lineage or the owner's release; key "the same plan item" and every carry on lineage. Still escapable by an undeclared copy into another plan.

**Required discriminator.** At the real owners (Derivation's `same`; the Interface's review-, re-review- and plan-brief constructors): admit `NO_PASS (X1)` under `A`; remove `A` and add `A2` whose heading differs by one byte (repeat with one prose byte elsewhere, and with one tightened contract field), and a re-dispatched `X2` with `X1`'s changed-unit content. Under (1): a peer's `PASS (X2)` is vetoed, `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` is derived and the review brief carries `X1`'s `NO_PASS`; under (2): §32.2 states the escape. For the plan: admit `REJECTED (o, {A: a1})`, make the same edit, request whole-plan acceptance: under (1) the brief carries the rejection. An implementation that keys any reach or carry on the part id or `item` key must fail. The diagnostic model cannot see this case: its review item revisions are `i1`, `i2` (an amendment of `I`) and `k1` (`I` re-identified with `i1`'s prose and contract), with no re-identified-and-amended revision, and its oracle restates the row's disjunction (N4).

## Non-blocking findings

**N1 — The relief disclosure names a lift that is also inadmissible in some of the states it covers (§5.10 (b) plan row, §32.2; 5895687 N2, partly closed).** The new text says that where relief is inadmissible (`r ⋢ cur`) "the obligation it derives ends by supersession of the rejection or an upheld challenge". The row's own supersession column admits a supersession of the rejection only while its basis holds (each named item revision `⊑ cur`). Where both `r ⋢ cur` and `r* ⋢ cur` (a prose amendment of the item after an accepted and a rejected incomparable branch; a removed item after a re-identification), only route 4 remains, which requires the owner to uphold a challenge against a rejection the owner may judge right; otherwise the derived `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` stays open indefinitely on work that is no longer issuable. (g)4 holds only through route 4. Repair (subtractive in wording): say "ends by an upheld challenge, or by supersession while the rejection's own basis holds"; or, for the stakeholder and subtractive, derive the plan row's `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` only while the vetoed acceptance covers `cur`.

**N2 — The plan row's justification no longer matches its cross-item reach (§5.10 (b), §13.4; editorial).** The row says the two judgments "contradict exactly when `r` and `r*` have a common tightening, which by §13.4 is when …", but `⊑` is defined within one item against its accepted revision and holds the `item` key fixed, so revisions of two items never have a common tightening in §13.4's sense; the row then excepts the `item` key. The reach is coherent as "a common tightening up to item identity"; say that. Related, both directions harmless but unstated: `depends_on` (and `objective.ref` anchors) also name items, so a re-identified dependency makes an unchanged item's revision a new target, and across plans byte-equal `depends_on` names different items and is reached (restrictive, liftable).

**N3 — Two different keys for "what was judged" (§5.10 (b)).** The plan row compares prose and every fixed field; the review row's cross-item disjunct compares prose and the `[contract]` table only (§13.1), so `kind`, `governing` revisions, `acceptance_criteria`, `gates` and `affected` do not enter it. A `NO_PASS` under item `K` therefore reaches identical content under an item whose governing units are pinned at other revisions (restrictive, liftable by relief). Either intended or an omission; state which. Option (1) of B1 removes the question.

**N4 — The model's review oracle restates the row's formula (§5.10 (g), §31.3; testing owner's proxy-proof rule; PEM DS-001).** `oracle_same` for `REV` returns "same content and (same item or same prose-and-contract)", the row's disjunction; the scenario space has no re-identified-and-amended item revision, so a green run coexists with B1. §31.3 says of the plan oracle that "a formula that disagrees with the claim fails"; nothing comparable is true for the review row. §31.3 also says both "of a re-identified item" and "the plan family has two items" (it has three, `A`, `B`, `A2`), and §5.10 (g) still says "coverage of two items". Add the missing case and correct the counts; state that the review oracle is the formula.

**N5 — Citation lag (editorial; 4c52205 N1 recurring).** The §5.10 heading lists SD-24 to SD-32 and the §0.3 closing paragraph says "the stakeholder decisions SD-11 to SD-32 are cited where they apply", while the body now cites SD-33.

**N6 — Cross-plan reach leaves the owner unnamed (§5.10 (a), (b) plan and review rows; new with SD-33).** "The rejecter's or the owner's" relief and `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` "for the owner" are defined by the actor policy for "that target's domain"; when a rejection in plan `P` reaches an acceptance in plan `Q` whose policy names another adjudicator, the text does not say whether the owner is `P`'s target's or `Q`'s. An implementer must choose. State it (for example: the owner of the adverse judgment's target for the obligation, either for relief).

**N7 — The rejection's denial premise is false for some grounds (5895687 N5, retained as a stakeholder trade-off).** Unchanged: a rejection grounded in looseness or infeasibility vetoes every peer's acceptance of the natural cure; owner- or rejecter-only. Under SD-33 the same now applies across items and plans.

**N8 — Retained trade-offs (stakeholder options, not blockers):** bounded-only reproduction (partly narrowing SD-17); SD-24's provisional cap and the gate minimum; fresh D1/D2 re-gating after fences, overtaken re-application and replacement iterations (R4, R12); withheld cross-pending-frame support; adverse frame judgments surviving abandonment; the fenced-cutover half state; context-revert reuse after supersession; two-sided, re-producer and unknown-authorship `authors` sets and their migration cost; an `INCOMPLETE` on a large scope widening every closure inside it; fixed `sat` hop direction; R2 plan amendments as new targets carried only in the brief; SD-32's veto of never-joined control-only branches; SD-33's cross-item and cross-plan veto of byte-identical items; and the paraphrase-only 2026-10-12 adoption instruction.
