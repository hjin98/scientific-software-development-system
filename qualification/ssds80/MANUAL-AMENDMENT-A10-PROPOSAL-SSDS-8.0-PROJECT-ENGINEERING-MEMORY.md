## 1. Why a re-derivation, and what the Manual already does

The handoff's amendment was reviewed against the SSDP 8.0 consolidated workplan: TaskEnvelope and ResultEnvelope fields, a reducer, a §9 typed graph and §21 materiality facts. That design was archived byte-identically (`513da27c`, 2026-10-01) and replaced by the SSDS 8 graph-native architecture, whose owners differ: work is obligations (§10) and items with work contracts (§11, §13); what a task owes is compiled into its brief (§16.3) and returned in typed slots (§13.5); facts come from a pure derivation over the ledger and content (§5); every decision owed with no report behind it goes to a principal as a `COORDINATE` decision (§12.5, §15.5). The amendment's own reopen trigger ("control plane narrowed: re-derive A, B and D against the narrowed design rather than re-adding machinery") applies. Nothing below is folded from the handoff by reference.

The accepted Manual already realizes part of the PEM contract:

| Accepted text | What it does |
|---|---|
| §0.4 S8, T3 | PEM is non-authoritative; a HAS is task-local; memory text is data |
| §16.3 injection table, PEM row | on "the item kind is `MIGRATE`, or the task replaces mature machinery (policy-tagged units), or recurrence detection", the brief carries the PEM owner text and `HISTORICAL` items |
| §16.2 `history(x)`, `HISTORICAL` label | ledger history and PEM entries offered only when the PEM predicate fires or on request |
| §15.7 | recurrence detected mechanically creates a `COORDINATE` decision carrying the convergence duty |
| §29.5 maintenance-audit row | no PEM admission or authority by assertion |
| §31.10 | PEM schema validation for the bound basis is a G1 document check |

Phase E realized the row for `MIGRATE` and recurrence (reading E13; `ssds.interface.render`). What remains at E3 today is the rest of the PEM contract: whether memory activates on the occasion (the clauses' qualifiers), retrieval and the HAS, freshness of a HAS against memory and owner changes, reverse reconciliation when an owner cited by an `AUTHORITY_BOUND` capability or normative notice changes, and the closeout learning assessment. The 7.x evidence that motivated the handoff stands (conditional "read the owner when …" routing loaded the owner in 15 of 41 owed cases; `qualification/ssdp70/PROTOCOL-7X-QUALIFICATION-STUDY-EVIDENCE-AND-LESSONS-2026-10-12.md` §2, §3.2), and §29.1 already names typed slots as the default form for owed questions.

## 2. Governing parents

**Manual.** S8 and T3; S5 (absence of a relation proves independence only inside judged-complete scope); S9 and A6 (no machine vocabulary turns a judgment into a fact; facts by machinery, judgment in typed slots); A4 (a duty a task owes is carried in its brief or enforced mechanically; briefs carry only triggered duties); A7 (no control decision parses prose); C3 and C7 (external facts only as records; minimum control data); P3 (derived state is rebuildable; canonical records win); T2 (unknown artifacts are never executed or deserialized to classify them); §5.1 (policy parameterizes, never adds a rule language); §16.4 (typed control facts in records, narrative by content reference); §33 (no registries, wrappers or fields solely to mirror inherited doctrine).

**PEM owners at the 7.2.1 public source** (both unchanged from 6.6.0; this proposal changes neither):

- *Activation* ("When memory activates"): memory loads only when demonstrated history can materially change the decision: substantial rework of mature D1-D4 semantics or concretization; replacement or consolidation of mature machinery; suspected recurrence; substantial optimization or scaling; migration, recovery, revert or restoration where prior choices matter; or an active workplan explicitly binding relevant memory. A first clean local defect, trivial or unrelated work, or the mere presence of a PEM file does not load it.
- *Retrieval and HAS*: resolve the accepted/base PEM by project Git acceptance policy, compose a validated candidate overlay (visibly candidate, never erasing accepted entries by omission), search canonical family and notice metadata regardless of temperature or summary, record the canonical HAS (`pem_basis` with exactly `accepted_project_state`, `accepted_pem`, `candidate_overlay_semantic_candidate`; rows `id`/`disposition`/`reason`), build a capability-transfer map when mature machinery is replaced, and refresh dispositions when the memory basis, overlay or a governing owner materially advances before integration or closeout.
- *Missing memory is not absence*: for missing, malformed, unsupported, partial or stale memory, "perform bounded historical intake over the affected scope **or** keep explicit `REVIEW_REQUIRED` uncertainty"; PEM is not a runtime single point of failure.
- *Authority binding*: `AUTHORITY_BOUND` force comes only from the exact current owner; an unhealthy or unavailable binding is `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, not retained force. The reverse reconciliation duty on owner change is in the workflow, architecture and evidence owners the schema owner routes to.
- *Closeout learning*: after an accepted material repair, rework, optimization, revert or restoration, the closeout learning assessment precedes lifecycle closure; updates only through admission thresholds and PEM governance; no specialist self-promotes or declares `AUTHORITY_BOUND`.
- *Scope and portability*: copying or forking memory does not manufacture local incidence; inherited entries are external or historical evidence until project governance reconciles lineage.

### 3.2 What the re-derivation buys and costs

It adds two contract fields (`memory`, `change_intent`), three slots, two `COORDINATE` triggers, one `HUMAN_ATTENTION` trigger, one analyzer, one policy parameter (`pem_root`), one proposed default (the candidate cap) and five `OBLIGATION` templates. It adds no record kind, obligation kind, component or store. The handoff's projection, `facts_state`, mismatch handling, rebuild obligation, discovery obligation, memory-review obligation, HAS-refresh obligation and `pem_uncertainty` are not needed, because each is replaced by derivation the Manual already performs or by an existing route.

The cost is mainly burden: an answer per fired occasion, a HAS whenever memory activates, and, rarely, an iteration spent when memory or a bound owner changes under in-flight work. §32.3 gains reopen triggers for both.

### 4.1 New §16.11 (inserted after §16.10)

> #### 16.11 Project Engineering Memory [D3 decision; amendment A10]
>
> This section is the one owner of how SSDS realizes the governing PEM contract: which of its occasions machinery detects, what a task owes when one fires, what machinery derives from memory, who accepts what machinery cannot decide, and what follows. PEM meaning (the activation predicate and its qualifiers, retrieval, the HAS shape, binding meanings, admission, maturity and claim strength) stays with the PEM owners at the governing source (S8). Nothing here adds a PEM field, a memory store or a judgment, and every other section that touches memory points here. This section fixes capabilities, holders, force and acceptance boundaries; the reader's exact facts and failure causes, the revert predicate, route resolution before genesis, term normalization and ranking, slot schemas, decision keys and outcome lists are catalogue §16.11 mechanism.
>
> **(a) Memory is content; its acceptance is recorded; its facts are derived.** Policy names the PEM subject, the root unit and its partition units (`pem_root`, §5.1), by default the unit that owns the project's discovery root. With none named, no PEM fact is derived and every PEM duty stays at the level the injected PEM owner text gives it (E3). The **accepted PEM publication** is the revision of that subject named by its latest standing acceptance in its current tenure: an `ACCEPTANCE` of a `NATIVE` subject, or, for a `LEGACY(p)` or `UNGOVERNED` subject, a `LEGACY_ACCEPTANCE` citing the project's own PEM acceptance evidence (§19.2), each by the actor policy designates under PEM governance (§3.5). It is never inferred from the observed tree, a path or a parse: an absorbed foreign change (§8.6) or a candidate changes no accepted publication, and the two coincide only where the project's acceptance process makes them coincide. With no such acceptance there is no accepted publication. PEM content that differs from the accepted publication, whether observed in the admitted state or a candidate's in a frame `D_n[X]` (§7), is a **candidate overlay**: composed against the publication only when it validates, visibly candidate, unable to erase an entry by omission, and seen only where it is observed. A pinned, data-only **PEM reader**, an analyzer in the sense of §5.9 that never executes content or instantiates objects from it (T2), reads the accepted publication's content, which the ledger keeps reachable (§21.4), and any overlay. It derives the facts the decisions below need: for every family and notice its identity, kind, state, authority binding, owner routes, normative status, applicability (tags, and typed bindings where the schema defines them) and lineage; its **record identity**, a digest of its complete canonical record; and the **reader state**, `READABLE`, or `UNREADABLE` with a cause (no accepted publication, absent, unparseable, unsupported schema, partial or conflicting composition). These **PEM facts** are S3: cached only in the derived index, rebuilt like derived relations, never stored in a record (P3). No copy of memory exists, so none can disagree with it. The reader is not schema authority: what it cannot read with certainty makes it `UNREADABLE`, never a guess. When the publication's root names another project than the one genesis records (§19.9), every entry is **foreign**: shown as external or historical evidence, never an owner-bound or typed candidate, its owner routes external, until project governance reconciles lineage and an accepted publication records it.
>
> **(b) Occasions.** Machinery detects these occasions of the PEM activation predicate, each in its own clause's words:
>
> | Occasion | Detected from | Effect |
> |---|---|---|
> | a plan item explicitly binds relevant memory | the item contract's `memory.bind` (§13.1), whose relevance plan acceptance judged | memory **activated** |
> | migration | item kind `MIGRATE`, or `change_intent` contains `migration` | **assessment** |
> | rework of mature semantics or concretization; replacement or consolidation of mature machinery; optimization or scaling | `change_intent` contains `rework`, `replacement`, `consolidation`, `optimization` or `scaling`; or the write scope intersects units policy tags `mature` | **assessment** |
> | revert or restoration | `change_intent` contains `revert` or `restoration`; or, on the candidate, a unit of its effect-keyed write set returns to a revision it held at an earlier admitted position other than its current one, or the candidate restores a subject (§19.4) | **assessment** |
> | recurrence | recurrence detection (§15.7), on the `COORDINATE` decision it creates | **assessment** |
> | any further occasion policy adds as a trigger (§5.1), for example a non-empty tag match | policy | **assessment** |
>
> An assessment occasion never activates memory by itself; it requires the task to judge the clause's qualifier ("substantial", "mature", "where prior choices matter"). Occasions machinery cannot detect stay agent judgment under the injected owner text (E3): suspected recurrence that detection has not found; history that can materially change a decision; a revert, restoration or recovery neither declared nor byte-identical. An agent may activate memory on any task where the predicate's own conditions hold, and returns a HAS when it does.
>
> **(c) What a task owes.** When any occasion fires, the brief carries the PEM owner text (§16.3), the reader state, and the candidates of (d) as `HISTORICAL` items with their match path and binding state, and the result owes two slots (§13.5). `pem_activation` holds one answer per fired assessment occasion: activates or not, with a content reference to the reason. `historical_applicability` is owed when the binding occasion fires or any answer activates: a content reference to the HAS in its canonical shape (in the durable home the governing work names, otherwise the result envelope), whose typed part is the three `pem_basis` identities and each dispositioned entry's identity and disposition; reasons stay in the home (§16.4). `HARD` `OBLIGATION` templates check that every fired occasion has an answer, that the HAS dispositions every candidate the recorded brief listed (§16.4), that its basis identities are the accepted publication and overlay of the state it was made in, and its freshness (f). They establish presence, coverage, identity and freshness only; whether a judgment, a disposition or the search behind it is adequate is review's (E3). Candidates are a floor: the bounded canonical-metadata search the PEM owner requires is still owed whenever memory is activated. An occasion detected only on the candidate (a revert, a restoration) triggers the slots at conformance, and a result without them is `INCOMPLETE` (§14.4).
>
> **(d) Candidates.** In a state, a task's candidates are: (1) the entries its contract binds; (2) **owner-bound** entries: `AUTHORITY_BOUND` families, owner-recorded `PROPOSED_FOR_PROMOTION` families and normative notices whose owner route resolves (e) to a unit the brief carries as `GOVERNING` (§16.2), whatever their binding state; (3) **typed matches**, where the schema defines owner or surface bindings: those resolving to a unit of the task's subjects or write scope (mechanism and regime bindings are shown, never matched, because SSDS holds no mechanism or regime facts); (4) **tag matches**: exact equality, after the catalogue's normalization, between the contract's `memory.terms` and an entry's applicability tags. Match-path candidates (3, 4) are capped (§0.6), selected by a deterministic function of the terms and the PEM facts; a truncation is shown with the omitted count, and the result says whether the agent widened its search. Bound and owner-bound candidates are never truncated and never count against the cap. In a frame, the candidates are the union of those derived from the accepted publication and from the overlay, each entry the overlay changes or retires marked so. Foreign entries are never owner-bound or typed candidates. Completeness is declared only for owner-bound candidates and only over resolved routes; an empty candidate set is rendered "no candidate", never "no applicable memory" (S5).
>
> **(e) Owner bindings and reverse impact.** An owner route **resolves** when it names this project and its path and section locate a governed unit (§1.11); the entry is then bound to that unit at the content the route's revision gives it. A route that names this project and locates no unit is **unresolved**; one naming another project is **external**. A binding's **reference position** is the admitted position of the acceptance whose publication first carried the entry's current record identity. In the admitted state a resolved binding is:
>
> - **stale** iff, at its reference position or some later admitted position, the owner unit's subject was valid (§5.5) at content that is neither the bound content nor reached from it by a path of current `EDITORIAL` or `REPRESENTATION` equivalence judgments (§4.4): an accepted owner change happened, whatever the owner's content is now, so a change later restored still counts;
> - **current** iff it is not stale and the owner's subject is valid now at the bound content or content so reached;
> - **review required** otherwise: the owner subject is not valid now, or only provisionally, or the binding has not yet been checked against any accepted owner state.
>
> The test reads admitted history through historical views (§5.8), a bounded derived query over the ledger, so it does not depend on the reader having been readable in between. Machinery decides only byte identity, so every owner change without such an equivalence counts (failing closed). In a frame, a binding to content the candidate changes, or to an owner valid there only by a prospective acceptance, is review required: candidate content never makes an authority binding current. Only a current binding is shown as healthy; every other state is shown wherever the entry appears (candidates, `HISTORICAL` items, `memory(x)`), and the PEM owner's rule that an unhealthy binding carries no retained force applies. Reverse impact covers every `AUTHORITY_BOUND` family, owner-recorded `PROPOSED_FOR_PROMOTION` family and normative notice of the accepted publication whose route resolves, whether or not any task is open: each stale binding derives a `COORDINATE` decision for the standing plan's principal (§12.5; the human where there is none), keyed on the entry, the owner unit and the position at which it went stale, whose brief carries the PEM owner and its schema-governance owner, with outcomes `AMEND` (whose plan change follows in the same request, §15.5: an item that reconciles the entry through PEM governance by confirming, remapping, downgrading or retiring it), `CHALLENGE` (against the owner change) or `ESCALATE`. Answering the decision does not make the binding current: only a later accepted publication that changes the entry's record moves its reference position, and a later owner change derives a new decision. Reverse reconciliation for owner-recorded `PROPOSED_FOR_PROMOTION` families is stronger than the inherited duty, which names `AUTHORITY_BOUND` entries and normative notices; it gives a proposal no force. A candidate that would make a binding stale lists it in its review brief (§16.5) and in `impact(diff)`. External and unresolved bindings, and any part of an interval that admitted history does not hold (before genesis), stay under the inherited reverse-reconciliation duty (E3).
>
> **(f) Freshness.** An entry's **dependency identity** is a digest of its record identity, the root fields that qualify every entry (schema, coverage, accepted base and overlay identity), and the states the reader derives for its routes (binding states under (e); evidence-route availability where retained content lets it decide). A HAS is **fresh** in a state iff every entry it dispositions and every entry that would now be a candidate of the task has there the dependency identity it had at the HAS's basis; an entry new since then is not. A material change to an entry (its claim, evidence rows or assessments, coverage, maturity, guidance or binding health) therefore stales exactly the HASes that depend on it, and a change to an unrelated entry stales none. Dependencies the reader cannot establish mechanically (an external evidence artifact, a material change outside memory) stay with the PEM owner's refresh duty (E3). A `HARD` `OBLIGATION` template checks freshness in the state the result is evaluated in (§14.1): the candidate's frame, re-derived after every clean rebase and at every revalidation of an integration request, including the one after an absorption (§11.5, §8.3 step 9); for a result without a candidate, the admitted head at its admission. A stale HAS makes the result `INCOMPLETE` and takes that class's route (§14.4); the next iteration's brief names the entries that changed. Because integration requires clean conformance on the rebased candidate (§8.3 step 7), a memory or bound-owner advance before integration cannot close work on an obsolete HAS. After integration the freshness verdict of the integrated enactment stands as decided: later memory reopens no closed work, while the obligation's ordinary terms still follow §10.4.
>
> **(g) Memory unavailable.** The reader state that counts is the one in the state the result is evaluated in; the brief states the state at issue, and if memory recovers or breaks during a task, that evaluation decides. While it is `UNREADABLE`, no candidate is derived, and a slot owed under (c) or (h) may be `NONE` with the typed reason `PEM_UNAVAILABLE`, and only then: the template compares the reason with the derived reader state, so the claim is corroborated by derivation, never by assertion. Such an answer states an uncertainty that only an independent judgment or a human accepts:
>
> 1. where an independent review judges the result's work (a transformation, item-level or integrated-result review), its brief carries the uncertainty as a required review item (§16.5), and a passing judgment of it is admissible only with that review's typed acceptance of the uncertainty (a result-only `HARD` template, §8.3 step 7); a review that rejects it does not pass;
> 2. for every other result (records-only work, a coordination decision, a judgment), `HUMAN_ATTENTION` is derived, keyed on that result and its stated uncertainty (§10.2), and an authenticated `HUMAN_DECISION` accepts or rejects it.
>
> Until the uncertainty is accepted, the result answers no obligation and supports no discharge (§10.3, §10.4, §15.5); the obligation is shown waiting on that review or that human (§10.3); work that does not depend on it proceeds, so memory is never a single point of failure. A rejection leaves the obligation to be answered again. An acceptance binds that one uncertainty and decision: the accepting review's or human's own record owes no further acceptance of it. While the reader is `READABLE`, the PEM owner's other fallbacks remain the agent's: dispositions may be `REVIEW_REQUIRED`, and partial coverage is handled by bounded intake or stated uncertainty (E3).
>
> **(h) Closeout learning.** The results of an item whose kind is `REPAIR`, whose `change_intent` contains `rework`, `replacement`, `consolidation`, `optimization`, `scaling`, `revert` or `restoration`, or whose candidate carries a detected revert or restoration owe the slot `closeout_learning`: `NOT_MATERIAL` (a first clean local defect with no recurrence or generalization signal, or no material change), `NO_UPDATE`, `UPDATE_PROPOSED` (a content reference to the proposed memory change) or, under (g) and with (g)'s acceptance, `NONE` with `PEM_UNAVAILABLE`; each but the last with a content reference to its reasoning. `migration` alone does not owe it, because the PEM owner's closeout clause does not name it. No answer writes, admits or promotes memory. An under-classified change (a material repair answered `NOT_MATERIAL`, a rework planned without an intent) is for review and for later recurrence detection; the risk is accepted (§32.2).
>
> **(i) Limits.** Machinery never judges applicability, a qualifier, recurrence, materiality, adequacy or admission; never writes, admits or promotes memory; never treats observed or candidate content as accepted memory or an accepted owner; never copies lesson text into a record or brief beyond entry identities, digests, enumerations and content references; and never derives from a PEM entry an obligation that blocks or closes a D1-D4 subject. ## 7. Qualification discriminators to carry to G2

Each is a case a shallow realization passes and a correct one fails. Mechanical properties need executable cases at the real owners; adequacy needs review.

1. A `MIGRATE` item, a `change_intent` of `optimization`, a write scope touching a `mature`-tagged unit, and a recurrence decision each require one `pem_activation` answer per occasion; an item with none of these requires none, and its brief carries no PEM owner text.
2. A `memory.bind` contract requires `historical_applicability` whatever `pem_activation` says; an all-`false` assessment with reasons requires no HAS.
3. A HAS missing a disposition for one listed candidate makes the result `INCOMPLETE`; coverage is checked against the recorded brief's candidates, not a recomputed set.
4. A candidate that reverts a unit to an earlier admitted revision raises the revert occasion at conformance and owes `closeout_learning`; a partial revert does not (disclosed, §32.2).
5. An accepted PEM change to a dispositioned entry, or a new entry that becomes a candidate, after the result's admission and before integration, makes the rebased candidate `INCOMPLETE`; the same change after integration reopens nothing; an unrelated PEM change makes nothing stale.
6. An owner unit change without an equivalence makes its bindings stale and derives one `COORDINATE` decision per binding; an `EDITORIAL` equivalence on the hop leaves it current; a second owner change derives a new decision.
7. An owner change made while the PEM root is unparseable produces the stale binding once the root parses again; so does a change and its restoration (A→B→A) in that interval (repair B5).
8. A route naming another project is external and never an owner-bound candidate; a PEM root naming another project makes every entry foreign (this repository's PEM today).
9. `PEM_UNAVAILABLE` is rejected while the reader is `READABLE`, accepted while `UNREADABLE`, and appears as a required review item; an item owing no PEM slot proceeds while memory is unreadable.
10. Owner-bound candidates survive any cap; match-path truncation is shown with the omitted count.
11. In a frame whose overlay retires or narrows a base entry, that entry is still a candidate, marked.
12. `REPAIR` with `NOT_MATERIAL` derives nothing; `UPDATE_PROPOSED` derives a principal decision and writes nothing; `migration` alone owes no `closeout_learning`.
13. No ledger record, brief section or derived-index entry contains lesson text, and removing every memory template leaves every non-memory admission decision unchanged.
14. Semantic (review): a trivial revert answered `activates: false` with a sound reason passes review; a decisive untagged entry missing from a HAS with an empty candidate set fails review; a complete but wrong HAS passes the templates and fails review.
