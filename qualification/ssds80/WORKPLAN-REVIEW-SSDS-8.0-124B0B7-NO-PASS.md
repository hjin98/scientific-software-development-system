## 1. Blocker by earliest owner

### A — withdrawing adverse currency can increase support without resolving the disagreement

**Primary trajectory: a challenge against the failing assessment.**

1. Take an unchanged claim subject `s`, evidence specification, regime and parameter instance. Admit competent assessments `a+` and `a−` of the same assessment key: PASS and FAIL. Neither supersedes the other; no `IMMATERIALITY` judgment exists. All required positive acceptances, gates and evidence are otherwise present. This is the starting state expressly required by §22's Assessment disagreement discriminator.
2. Their bases hold. The positive records need not rely on `a−`: they can predate it. Initially `current(a+) = T`, `current(a−) = T`, `undisputed(e) = P`, so `valid(s) ≤ P` (§6.6 S2).
3. A qualified actor files an ordinary **blocking challenge** `c` targeting the support-tier assessment `a−`. The target is permitted and strictly earlier. Its target is not a restrictive adjudication, so it is not a counter-challenge. No adjudication, withdrawal, supersession, discriminating reassessment or immateriality decision follows (§6.6 S1, §6.8 steps 3 and 5).
4. S1 now gives `status1(a−) = BLOCKED`. S2 consequently gives `base(a−) = F` and `current(a−) = F`. The claim triple itself is not challenged; the independent positive support still has value `T` (§6.6).
5. `undisputed(e)` tests only failing assessments whose currency is `T` or `P`. There is now none. It returns `T`; the positive evidence term and `valid(s)` can return `T`, and §7.4 can classify the dependent obligation `DISCHARGED`.
6. The challenge's own adjudication obligation can remain open while the evidence-conflict obligation ceases to be live. Neither §6.6 nor §7.4 makes resolving that challenge a prerequisite of the positive claim's unqualified closure. Displaying the challenge elsewhere does not change these predicates (§6.6 S3, §9.3).

This uses no forged key, bad lineage, stale claim, changed execution dependency, foreign push or illicit supersession. Filing a challenge questions the adverse assessment; it does not establish the contrary assessment correct or resolve the underlying material disagreement. A conscientious reviewer may refuse closure, but the claimed deterministic protection must also constrain locally compliant actors.

**The polarity reversal.** S1 calls every ordinary challenge restrictive. Here its suspension of `a−` is permissive *for the dependent claim*, because S2 reads `current(a−)` through inhibition. The graph contains `valid(s) → current(a−)` and the independent positive-support edges, with no path back. It remains acyclic. Topological evaluation uniquely yields the unwanted `T`; cycle detection cannot repair the semantic omission (§6.6, §6.8 step 5).

**Recovery sibling: the same reversal without a challenge.**

1. Retain both assessments and their still-unresolved conflict at head `R`. Lose an admitted suffix, restore ledger and product consistently to `R`, and admit the required `LEDGER_LOSS`. Keep the claim, specification, regime and execution bytes unchanged (§15.4).
2. The boundary withholds every pre-loss assessment and realization through `cont`; it makes `current(a−) = F`, rather than preserving its new restrictive effect (§6.6 S2, §15.4's polarity table).
3. Requalify the positive realization, passing assessment and acceptance after the boundary, with no supersession of `a−` and no judgment resolving its applicability or material disagreement. Alternatively, a continuity attestation can hold an explicit set of positive records and their premises without holding `a−`; §15.4 permits explicit record sets.
4. The old negative assessment remains retained but loss-withheld. `undisputed(e) = T`, so fresh/held positive support can make the claim unqualified. Positive requalification or a limited attestation is not one of §10's conflict-resolution operations.

The boundary is allowed to withhold affirmative warrant. It must not thereby treat a known unresolved disagreement as resolved. This finding does **not** require treating uncertain or stale adverse results as admissible refutation: uncertainty can remain a restriction pending explicit owner assessment. The [evidence owner, Applicability][evidence] provides `review-required` for uncertain applicability.

**Further affected assumption.** §15.4 says supersessions survive because none can relax anything. With inhibitory assessments, superseding `a−` removes the cap, so that justification is no longer general. This Review does not claim that every previously authorized supersession should be undone; the D3 owner must explicitly reconcile which effects remain safe across recovery, instead of inheriting support-only polarity arguments (§6.6, §15.4).

**What it violates, with owner text.** The candidate's §10 permits disagreement resolution:

> “only by the owner's supersession or an `IMMATERIALITY` judgment”

Neither occurred in the primary trajectory. §6.6's Assessment disagreement paragraph likewise names explicit owner resolution; `undisputed` instead removes the qualification through generic currency suppression. P5 requires that “restrictive effects survive” a known loss (§2.4); §15.4 withholds the adverse assessment even though it now supplies such an effect.

The accepted [evidence owner, Assessment disagreement][evidence] requires:

> “An unresolved material disagreement keeps dependent present guidance/closure visibly qualified.”

Its Applicability section also says to use `review-required` when applicability is uncertain. Suspended or loss-withheld adverse evidence is not affirmative proof that the disagreement ceased to matter. The [definition owner, Source and context availability][defs] forbids unresolved simultaneously applicable conflicting meanings; its well-definedness section requires material state and branch semantics. The support-only recovery explanation and the new inhibitory predicate cannot both establish the claimed conservative behavior.

**Earliest owner.** D3 assessment applicability/inhibition and its composition with generic record challenges and recovery: §6.6 S1/S2, §7.4, §10 and §15.4. Acyclicity itself is not the faulty mechanism. This is not a missing D4 helper or a defect in accepted evidence doctrine.

**Minimum repair direction.** Re-derive the effect of adverse assessments and their dispositions across challenges, provisionality, supersession, target/execution movement and known loss. Distinguish loss of positive warrant from resolution of a recorded disagreement. Until an authorized owner resolves or holds the adverse evidence inapplicable, a contested applicability/disposition must not manufacture unqualified support. Preserve the existing stale-evidence rule and explicit resolution routes. Use the existing records and derived obligations where sufficient; this finding requires no new store, registry or generic state machine.

Because the defect crosses mechanisms repeatedly repaired in prior revisions, apply [convergence, Active simplification trigger][convergence] before adding another exception. Its instruction is “simplification/re-derivation is mandatory before another additive durable repair.” Reconcile the family at one semantic owner rather than patching only the primary example.

**Required discriminators.** Through real Admission, Derivation, discharge and inspection consumers (§22):

- PASS plus competent FAIL; challenge FAIL with no adjudication: no unqualified dependent closure.
- Challenge the challenge's dismissal, and challenge an `IMMATERIALITY` judgment: unresolved uncertainty must retain qualification, including provisional dispositions.
- Known loss with both assessments retained; positive-only requalification and positive-only continuity attestation: no silent disagreement resolution.
- Contrast explicit authorized resolution, genuinely stale adverse evidence after a material basis change, different regimes, and ordinary uncontested passing evidence.
- Reverse assessment order and vary evaluation order; exercise fresh and retained supersession effects across the boundary.

The checks must not seed the conflict resolved or replace the real owner with a fixture's favored interpretation. [Testing, Proxy-proof real-owner evidence][testing] says an oracle that can stay green while that owner is broken cannot close the owner claim.
