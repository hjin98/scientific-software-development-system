## 1. Blockers by earliest owner

### A — §6.9's exact counting rule conflicts with permanent exclusion after a tenure ends

**Trajectory.** Choose a native composite subject `P` containing part `A`. There are no challenges, losses, domain changes, content edits or evidence failures.

1. `P` has current holistic acceptance `j_P`. An authorized task produces `X0`, changing only `A`'s declaration from part to subject. The scope and semantic revisions are unchanged, as in §22's structural-only promotion case.
2. The independent reviewer records prospective acceptance `j` in `D_n[X0]`, naming `(A, scope_id(A), rev(A))`. Its basis includes `j_P`, the judged revision and required context. Integration `k1` enacts the promotion and credits `j` to tenure `τ1` (§6.9, §12.2).
3. A later lawful admission demotes `A` back into a part of the same `P`. `τ1` ends. `P` retains its original scope, signature, tenure and unchanged bytes; `j_P` remains current. No record is superseded or challenged (§6.1, §12.2).
4. A new authorized task proposes restoration `X1`, again changing exactly the same declaration from part to subject. It has new base/candidate commits and a new task/result identity, but `Δ_Σ(X1) = Δ_Σ(X0)`. Choose the same bytes, scope and governing context. All of `j`'s content/context and record premises still hold in `D_n[X1]`.
5. The integration request references `j` rather than obtaining a new acceptance. New result, executions and structural review can be provided; those do not themselves supply a fresh acceptance judgment. The explicit prospective-acceptance predicate has both required conditions: equal structural delta and a holding basis.
6. At `k2`, the first branch of `ACC(A)` excludes `j` because it predates the new `τ2`. Its prospective branch nevertheless counts `j` under §6.9. Thus the same acceptance can supply `τ2`, despite having already supplied the ended `τ1`.

This is not ordinary early cutoff within one tenure. It is reuse of the acceptance itself across a governance lifecycle boundary. No stale content, forged lineage, foreign push or risk override is needed. The same conflict arises when `X1` is a purported clean rebase with an equal structural delta: equality does not prove that it is a continuation of the still-unenacted original proposal.

**What it violates, with owner text.** §6.9 says a prospective acceptance counts “exactly when two conditions hold,” then specifies equal `Δ_Σ` and a basis holding at integration. Neither condition distinguishes the first enactment from a second enactment after demotion. Conversely, §12.2 states:

> “A's acceptance judgments leave ACC(A) permanently”

and:

> “Restoring A to subject status later ... needs a new acceptance bound to the same admission.”

S10/S11 require acceptance in one tenure and prohibit revival (§2.1). A conforming implementer of the explicit predicate can violate those invariants; an implementer obeying the permanent-exclusion prose must add an unstated exception to the predicate. This is a material owner-local conflict, not permission to select whichever passage appears later.

The [definition owner, Source and context availability][defs] requires canonical meaning without conflicting simultaneously applicable statements. The [architecture owner, D3 formal-contract discipline][arch] requires lifecycle and transition semantics to be defined before dependent use. Those requirements are not satisfied by leaving this distinction to interpretation.

**Earliest owner.** The prospective-acceptance/tenure counting contract, §6.9 Integration/Rebase and §6.6 `ACC`, composed with §12.2. This is D3 lifecycle identity, not a missing D4 helper.

**Minimum repair direction.** Bind each prospective acceptance to one enactment lineage and the one tenure it can start. Permit applicable rebases before that enactment; after the bound tenure ends, exclude the acceptance permanently from every later tenure. Resolve this using the existing result/request/admission history if sufficient. Equal structural delta plus current content is necessary for reuse before enactment, but insufficient to establish lifecycle continuity. Reconcile `ACC`, frame currentness and restoration with that one rule.

**Required discriminator.** Through real owners: part → prospective acceptance → integration → demotion → identical restoration. Reuse of the first prospective acceptance must fail; a fresh independently qualified acceptance must succeed. Include an unrelated pre-integration rebase that keeps the first acceptance usable, so the repair does not forbid legitimate rebase reuse. Test structural-only transformations and quarantined-adoption restoration (§22).

### B — §6.6 and §7.4 can select a PASS from unresolved current disagreement

**Trajectory.** Choose an evidence specification and unchanged claim with two independently produced, admissible assessments. The disagreement is substantive, not a stale result or different parameter regime.

1. Qualified assessors record `a−` and `a+` on the same current claim/evidence obligation: one concludes the realized evidence fails the protected claim; the other concludes it passes. Their bases, custody and qualified lineages hold. They are independent assessments, not an explicitly authorized adjudication or a replacement of an earlier erroneous record.
2. Both records remain current. Neither explicitly supersedes the other. No S1 challenge has been raised; an adverse evidence assessment is a support-tier judgment, not automatically an S1 challenge (§6.3, §6.6, §10).
3. A passing acceptance/review and all other required gates exist. In §6.6 `valid(s)`, the disjunction over current admissible assessments can select `a+`; there is no term requiring disposition of `a−` or of the disagreement. In §7.4, a current qualified passing judgment plus current assessed evidence can likewise make the obligation `DISCHARGED`.
4. Admission's currency and support-acyclicity checks pass. Step 7 consumes the positive discharge predicate; step 8 can preserve existing closures. No defined rule identifies this conflict and makes adjudication a prerequisite of those closures (§6.8).

A policy could add a conflict gate, or a conscientious reviewer could raise a challenge. Neither is a mandatory system condition of these predicates. The candidate must preserve the governing evidence rule under its permitted policies; merely allowing a stronger policy is insufficient. Reading §10's “later reassessment supersedes” as automatic latest-record selection is also insufficient: serialization order cannot adjudicate competent material disagreement.

**What it violates, with owner text.** The accepted [evidence owner, Assessment disagreement][evidence] states:

> “An unresolved material disagreement keeps dependent present guidance/closure visibly qualified.”

It requires preservation and owner adjudication or discriminating evidence, and prohibits resolving material disagreement by serialized record position. It distinguishes explicit resolved reassessment from still-live disagreement. The candidate supplies immutable history and explicit supersession but not this closure condition (§6.6, §7.4, §10).

S4's assessment model (§2.1), C6's integrity boundary (§2.2), and the claimed lossless transfer of evidence/acceptance semantics (§18.1, §19) cannot be realized by an unconditional positive existential witness. Retaining the contrary assessment in a cold history view does not qualify the machine's current PASS.

**Earliest owner.** The candidate's evidence-support and canonical-closure contract: §6.6 S2, §7.4 and §10. Governing constraint: the accepted evidence owner's Assessment disagreement section. This is a D3 abstraction-adequacy defect; it does not challenge that accepted doctrine.

**Minimum repair direction.** Specify the bounded conflict/applicability judgment, its real decision owner and the propagation that prevents unqualified support/discharge until disposition. Preserve both assessments and explicit supersession/adjudication. Materiality may remain a qualified semantic judgment; once recorded, its consequences must be deterministic. Do not resolve by newest record, majority vote, automatic adverse-result deletion, or requiring every assessment disagreement to be restated as a separate Serious Challenge.

**Required discriminator.** Admit a current PASS and current adverse assessment with unresolved material disagreement; dependent closure must remain qualified/open. Reverse their ledger order. Contrast with a stale adverse assessment, assessments of explicitly different regimes, and an authorized adjudication or explicit resolved reassessment. Use the real Admission/Derivation/inspection consumers; no fixture may seed a resolved conflict (§22).
