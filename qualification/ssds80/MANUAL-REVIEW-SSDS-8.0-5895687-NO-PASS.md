## Blockers

### B1 — Re-identifying a plan item escapes a standing objection's veto and its brief carry (family: objection reach and disclosure keyed on plan-item identity; sibling of d6734c4 B1 and of the gate-kind 362af9f B1-A / 61cd09b B1 (ii))

**Text.** §5.10 (b), `PLAN_ACCEPTANCE` row: "a rejection of `r*` reaches every acceptance naming a revision **of the item** with the same prose and fixed fields, whoever recorded it and whenever (SD-32)". §5.10 (b), transformation `REVIEW` row (and `CONFORMANCE` "as `REVIEW` of a transformation"): reach is "the **same plan item** at any of its revisions with the same changed-unit content". §16.5: a plan-acceptance brief carries "every standing `PLAN_ACCEPTANCE` rejection that names **any item the acceptance asks about**"; a re-review brief carries "the earlier **iterations'** review findings that no later judgment resolved (… a later review of **the same item** …)". §11.1: "Each **item** is a section whose heading path the manifest declares as a part"; §4.3: a plan acceptance has "`content` on … each accepted item revision", that is `(u, r)` for the item's part unit `u`. §11.4: "item topology (`depends_on`, added or removed items) … always need whole-plan review". §5.10 (a): "Every reach contains the target; reaching further is what keeps amendment, rebase, re-dispatch, a new run or a composition of judgments from escaping an objection." §0.3 R9: "A review or acceptance disagreement is never resolved by re-dispatch, count or order." §32.2: "An amendment of a plan item's prose or fixed fields is a new target a standing rejection does not reach; the rejection is carried into the next acceptor's brief (§16.5)".

**Trajectory (a): plan rejection, conforming at every step.**

1. Plan `P` has item `A` (part unit `P.A`, revision `a1`). An authenticated independent human `H` records `PLAN_ACCEPTANCE REJECTED (o, {A: a1})`. It is never-lost and stands. A is not issuable.
2. An authenticated human (the plan's principal, or any human by ordinary change, §12.3 "any, as ordinary change") edits the plan unit and its manifest declaration: part `P.A` is removed and part `P.A2` declared (a new heading and part id, a new `item` key), whose item prose and contract are byte-identical to `a1`'s apart from that identity. (Whether an agent principal's write scope, "its plan's units only", admits the manifest edit does not matter here.) This is a topology change (removed and added items), so §11.4 requires whole-plan review and `PLAN_ACCEPT` is derived for `A2`. Nothing in §8.3 refuses it: a plan rejection is not a blocking challenge, so the step 4 (b) freeze does not apply, and no rule ties a part's removal to its standing objections.
3. A fresh independent reviewer `R` receives the whole-plan brief. §16.5 selects rejections naming an item the acceptance asks about; `j` names `A`, which is no longer in the plan, so it is not carried. Nothing else in §16.5-§16.7 requires it (the gate-brief listing of §16.6 is for gate decisions).
4. `R` records `ACCEPTED (o, {A2: a2})`. §5.10 (b): `j` reaches acceptances "naming a revision of the item"; `A2` is a different item, so `R`'s acceptance is not reached, not vetoed, and covers `cur(A2) = a2`. A2 is issuable. `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` is not derived (no acting passing judgment is in `j`'s reach).
5. Work proceeds on byte-identical item content that a standing, unsuperseded, unrelieved independent rejection denies. Neither the rejecter, nor the owner, nor the acceptor saw the rejection after it was recorded; the disclosure §32.2 promises for amended items ("carried into the next acceptor's brief") is not delivered. The only judgment after `j` is `R`'s, made without it: the rejection was overridden by a later peer's unrouted pass, the "latest-editor order" the evidence owner forbids.

Variant: the same with a copy of `A` into a new plan subject (a new `PLAN` unit): no item is shared, nothing is reached or carried. Variant with history: had `A` an earlier acceptance of a same-prose revision, that stale acceptance stays vetoed and an `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` about the removed item is derived; it does not reach `A2` and does not stop it being issued.

**Trajectory (b): transformation review, the sibling row.** Item `A`'s task produces `X1` changing unit `u` to content `C`; `H` records `REVIEW NO_PASS (X1)`. A human re-identifies `A` as `A2` as in (a); a fresh plan acceptor accepts it (there is no plan rejection to carry). The Dispatcher issues `A2`'s task; it produces `X2` with the same changed-unit content `C`. A fresh reviewer receives a brief without `H`'s findings (they are an earlier iteration of another item's obligation, §16.5) and records `PASS (X2)`; the `NO_PASS` reaches only "the same plan item", so the `PASS` is unvetoed and `X2` integrates the content a standing review rejected, by re-dispatch under a new identity, with no owner routing. This is exactly the shape the gate kind was repaired for after 362af9f B1-A ("Gate `REJECT` escaped by a different item reaching identical bytes"): the gate veto now keys on gate and subject triple in any run, but the review and plan rows still key on item identity.

**Why this is a defect, not a disclosed trade-off.**

- The reach rule's stated purpose (§5.10 (a)) and R9's ("never resolved by re-dispatch") are defeated by a locally compliant identity edit that changes nothing judged. The §32.2 residual for subject triples ("new subjects' acceptances are new targets it does not reach; their briefs show it only through the inspector's history") rests on a semantic reason (parts can be acceptable while the whole fails); a re-identified item with byte-identical prose and contract has no such reason, and §32.2 says nothing about plan-item re-identification.
- For plan rejections, the Manual's own disclosure (§32.2: "carried into the next acceptor's brief") and its selector (§16.5: "names any item the acceptance asks about") disagree on this input; the d6734c4 Review held the same kind of selector gap, after an objective-only amendment, to be a blocker; here the veto is missed as well, which d6734c4 B1 did not have.
- SD-32's verbatim text ("every acceptance naming a revision with identical prose and fixed fields") does not limit the reach to one item; the Manual's "of the item" is a narrowing of it that the Manual does not surface (handoff §1 (b)).

**Violated owner text.** Evidence owner (`source/shared/references/evidence-evolution-and-dependencies.md` at the public-source commit, line 153): "Do not resolve material assessment disagreement by majority vote, latest-editor order, reviewer seniority, count, temperature, or serialized record position." Workflow owner (`workflow-and-workplans.md`, line 61): "A current handoff must be snapshot-complete for its governed task scope: recover … unresolved blockers/Challenge … without hidden chat or unavailable history", and line 104: "A synthesizer reconciles findings through evidence and owner adjudication; it cannot erase unresolved contradictory material evidence by vote." Manual §0.4 A4 ("a duty a task owes is carried in its brief or enforced mechanically"); stakeholder SD-30 ("carry standing rejections into the next acceptor's brief") and SD-32 (verbatim above). Workplan §21 items 5 (judgment derivation) and 10 (brief templates).

**Earliest owner.** Manual §5.10 (b) (`PLAN_ACCEPTANCE` and transformation `REVIEW`/`CONFORMANCE` rows) and §16.5 (both carries); SD-32's item-scope reading belongs to the stakeholder. §32.2, §31.6 (V) and the closure map follow.

**Family assessment (convergence owner, "Review strategy after recurrence").** One cause: the reach and the carry name the thing judged by a mutable identity (the plan item's part id) rather than by what was judged (the item's prose and contract; the changed-unit content), and plan topology edits change that identity without any judgment of the objection. Siblings checked: plan rejection after re-identification (veto and carry missed: fails); plan rejection after a copy into another plan (fails, same cause); transformation `REVIEW`/`CONFORMANCE` `NO_PASS` after re-identification, or under another item whose task produces the same changed content (fails); gate `REJECT` through another item (held: keyed on the subject triple since the 362af9f repair); `ACCEPTANCE FAIL` through another item (held: keyed on the triple); `EQUIVALENCE`/`COMPLETENESS` (held: keyed on unit revisions and scopes, which a plan edit does not touch); objective-only and multi-item plan edits (held, d6734c4 B1 closed); prose amendments (the disclosed new-target residual, carried: held). I stopped there; the remaining identity routes (unit `renamed_from`, splits) are the same cause and their enumeration would be implementation work. It is a clean family with one cause, newly found, not a further disjunct of the plan-reach formula.

**Minimum repair directions (options; the first two subtract identity from the key).**

- **(1) Recommended: key on what was judged.** Plan row: a rejection of `r*` reaches every acceptance naming any item revision, of any item and plan, whose prose and fixed fields (item identity excluded) equal `r*`'s, which is SD-32's verbatim reading; transformation rows: "any transformation with the same changed-unit content under an item revision with the same prose and contract" (or simply the same changed-unit content, if the stakeholder accepts that a review of a change binds every item). Both carries select by the same key. Cost: two distinct items with byte-identical prose and contract cross-veto; route 3 lifts it with one record.
- **(2) Disclosure only (no veto change).** Carry into every whole-plan acceptance brief every standing plan rejection on the plan subject, removed items included, and into every review brief every standing `NO_PASS`/`NONCONFORMANT` whose changed-unit content equals the candidate's, whatever item; state in §32.2 that re-identified or copied items are new targets the veto does not reach. One existing query broadened per carry; the veto stays escapable, disclosed.
- **(3) Item lineage (adds mechanism).** Make plan item identity follow declared lineage (`renamed_from`) for reach and carry, and refuse an R2 topology edit that drops a part carrying a standing objection without a lineage declaration or the owner's release. Narrower than (1) and still escapable by an undeclared copy.

**Required discriminator.** At the real owners (Derivation's `same`, the Interface's plan-brief and review-brief constructors): admit `REJECTED (o, {A: a1})`; re-identify `A` as `A2` with byte-identical prose and contract; request whole-plan acceptance. Under (1): the brief carries `j`, a peer's `ACCEPTED (o, {A2: a2})` is vetoed, `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` is derived, and `H`'s or the owner's exact-target acceptance relieves it; under (2): the brief carries `j` and §32.2 states the residual. Repeat for a copy into a second plan and for trajectory (b) (`NO_PASS (X1)` under `A`, identical content under `A2`). An implementation that keys either reach or either carry on the part id alone must fail. The diagnostic model cannot express this (one identity per item, no topology edits; N4).

## Non-blocking findings

**N1 — The closed form names too few fixed fields (§5.10 (b), §0.3 R14, §13.4; SD-32's table row).** The row says `r` and `r*` have a common tightening "when their item prose is byte-identical and every field §13.4 marks 'unchanged' has the same value (each control field then has a tighter value common to both)". I re-derived the meet for every whitelisted control field and it holds (subset fields by intersection, superset fields and `HARD` secondaries by union, `budget` by minimum, `scientific` by `APPLIES`; feasibility is not claimed, so an empty intersection is still a revision). But §11.1's item fields include `affected` (and the `item` key), which §13.4's table never lists: they fall under "any other field … not in the whitelist: R2", so `⊑` fixes them too, yet they are not "marked 'unchanged'". Two revisions differing only in `affected` therefore have no common tightening, while the formula reaches them. The error is restrictive only (an extra veto, liftable by route 3) and the claim ("exactly when … a common tightening") resolves it; but two normative statements disagree on one input. Repair (subtractive in wording): "every field outside the tightening rows has the same value", or add `affected` and identity to the "unchanged" row; the same words in SD-32's decisions-table summary.

**N2 — Route-3 relief is admissible only while the relieved acceptance's basis holds, so the stated lift is not always available (§0.3 R14, §5.10 (d), §31.6 (V), §32.2).** A relief record is a passing `PLAN_ACCEPTANCE` on the vetoed acceptance's exact target; §8.3 step 6 admits it only if its `PLAN_CONTROL` entry (`r ⊑ cur`) and `EXACT` objective entry hold. With SD-32's static reach, a vetoed acceptance of `r` keeps deriving `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` in states where `r ⋢ cur`: for example a rejection of `a0`, an acceptance of the incomparable `aQ`, and the principal back at `a0` (or at a revision with other prose). There the owner who agrees with both judgments cannot relieve (`aQ ⋢ cur`) and need not supersede (`a0` may be rightly rejected); only route 4 (upholding a challenge against the rejection) or the principal's return to a revision `aQ` covers ends it. Invariant (g)4 still holds through route 4, and the model's I4 check uses exactly that route; but "until the rejecter or the owner relieves it" (§0.3 R14, §32.2) overstates the cost's lift. Repair: state the admissibility limit; or, put to the stakeholder and subtractive, derive the plan row's `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` only where the vetoed acceptance covers `cur`.

**N3 — "The rejected revision included" is true only when `r ⊑ r*` (§5.10 (b), (d) route 3, §32.2, closure map §5; editorial).** For incomparable `r` and `r*`, relieving the acceptance of `r` clears its coverage of their common tightenings, not of `r*`, which it never covered. Say "every tightening of `r`, among them every common tightening with `r*` (and `r*` itself when `r ⊑ r*`)".

**N4 — The plan oracle restates the formula over the modelled fields, and the model cannot see B1, N1 or N2 (§5.10 (g), §31.3; testing owner's proxy-proof rule; PEM DS-001).** `oracle_same` builds `meet = (pz, min(bz, bj), sz & sj)` and tests it below both revisions; with only prose, budget and scope, the minimum and intersection always lie below both, so the test is `pz == pj`, the row's formula. The rebuild does close the 4c52205 N5 point (the oracle no longer reads `cur`), and a formula reading `cur` or `⊑` would now fail it, which is useful; but the scenario space has no fixed field other than prose, no non-whitelisted field, one identity per item and no topology edit, and the model does not apply Admission step 6 to relief records. A green run coexists with B1, N1 and N2. Add to §5.10 (g) that the plan oracle distinguishes claim from formula only on prose, budget and scope.

**N5 — The rejection's denial premise is false for some grounds, so natural cures need the rejecter or the owner (stakeholder trade-off, disclosed in substance).** The row grounds "a rejection of `r*` denies every tighter revision" on "a tightening keeps the controls it tightens, so it keeps what was rejected". That holds for a rejection of what is asked, but not for one grounded in looseness (a write scope too broad, cured by narrowing it), and the reviewed workplan §7.9 says an acceptance claims nothing about feasibility ("It does not claim the work is still feasible"), so an acceptance of a looser revision does not contradict a rejection of `r*` as infeasible (a budget too small, cured by raising it). In both cases the cure has the same prose and fixed fields and every peer's acceptance of it is vetoed. Fail-closed, liftable, and within SD-31/SD-32's chosen cost; recorded so the stakeholder sees that the common cure paths for control-grounded rejections are owner- or rejecter-only. No repair suggested (any repair would add mechanism, such as a typed rejection ground).

**N6 — (g)6's parenthetical still understates order-dependent admission (4c52205 N6, unaddressed; editorial).** "where admission itself depends on order (a reference not yet present)": Admission also depends on order through step 6 currency (including plan supersession and relief admitted only while their basis holds, N2) and step 7 (`OBLIGATION_ANSWERED`). Widen or drop the parenthetical.

**N7 — Retained trade-offs (stakeholder options, not blockers), disclosed in §0.3, §32.2-§32.3 or closure map §5:** bounded-only reproduction (partly narrowing SD-17); SD-24's provisional cap and the gate minimum; fresh D1/D2 re-gating after fences, overtaken re-application and replacement iterations (R4, R12); withheld cross-pending-frame support; adverse frame judgments surviving abandonment; the fenced-cutover half state; context-revert reuse after supersession; two-sided, re-producer and unknown-authorship `authors` sets and their migration cost; an `INCOMPLETE` on a large scope widening every closure inside it; fixed `sat` hop direction; R2 plan amendments as new targets carried only in the brief; SD-32's veto of never-joined control-only branches from the rejection's admission; and the paraphrase-only 2026-10-12 adoption instruction.
