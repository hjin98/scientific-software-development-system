## 1. Blocker

**Text.** §5.10 (b), `PLAN_ACCEPTANCE` row (line 834): "a rejection denies each item revision it names, under any objective revision; an acceptance claims each item revision it names and every revision on the tightening path from it to the item's current revision (§13.4), through which its coverage passes, so a rejection of `r*` reaches an acceptance naming `r` when `r* = r` or `r ⊑ r* ⊑ cur`; acceptances do not compose". §13.4 (line 1493): `⊑` is componentwise over the contract fields with byte-identical prose; "a standing rejection of a revision on the path from an accepted revision to the current one reaches the acceptance whose coverage passes through it". §11.4: "control tightening keeps the existing plan acceptance current (R1)". §0.3 R14 states the reason for the rule: "one reaching only the current revision let a further tightening escape a standing rejection; the path rule (`r ⊑ r* ⊑ cur`) avoids both". **Trajectory (B1(a), incomparable acceptance).** One item, prose fixed throughout; only contract fields move.

## 2. Non-blocking findings

**N1 — The plan supersession currency condition closes routes 1-2 exactly where the new reach bites.** The row's supersession target is "the target, only while the rejected objective and item revisions are current; otherwise only an upheld challenge lifts it". Admission step 6 already refuses a superseding judgment whose basis does not hold (`content` on the rejected revision, class `PLAN_CONTROL`, holds when `r* ⊑ cur`). The added condition therefore bites only where `r* ⊑ cur` but `r* ≠ cur` (after a further tightening), where another named item of a multi-item rejection has changed, or after an objective revision change: precisely the cases the path rule and per-item denial newly reach. There the rejecter cannot withdraw and the owner cannot adjudicate by supersession; only a challenge plus an independent adjudication ends it. (g)4 holds through route 4, so this is a liveness cost, not a defect. Example: a rejection of `{A1, B1}` grounded in `B1`; `B` is fixed to `B2`; a fresh `ACCEPTED{A1, B2}` is vetoed (A1 is named), and nobody but an adjudicator of a raised challenge can lift it (or the principal makes a trivial tightening of `A`). Prefer deleting the condition (subtractive); state also whether it applies when an acceptance, rather than a rejection, is superseded (step 3 reads the column for both). The row's "lifts it" should say "restores issuance": the veto of the earlier acceptance and the derived `ADJUDICATE_DISAGREEMENT` persist.

**N2 — Fixed hop direction is now redundant and narrows the reviewed `sat` without a disclosed cost.** §4.4 now requires a hop `r_{i-1} -> r_i` to use a judgment with `r_from = r_{i-1}`, `r_to = r_i`. With `authors` orientation-free (the 34803df B2 repair), a reversed-endpoint judgment by an author is `nonind` either way, so the direction rule no longer closes anything. It does remove support the reviewed text gave (workplan §6.6: "An equivalence judgment relates two revisions of one unit"; the `sat` formula names no direction): an independent equivalence recorded with endpoints reversed, or one judged before a revert for consumers on the other side, serves no path the other way. R14 mentions the rule only in its "why" cell; its cost cell and §32.2 do not. Prefer removing it (subtractive) unless some class (perhaps `MIGRATION`) is directional, in which case say so in §4.4.

**N3 — Model scenario space and oracle.** §5.10 (g) now discloses honestly that the claims column is a shared definition the model cannot test (34803df N1 closed). Separately, the plan family's order (a chain plus one isolated revision) cannot generate a revision incomparable with a rejected one but below the current one, so B1 is outside its scenario space as well as outside its oracle. The equivalence family fixes per-revision authorship (`REV_AUTHORS`), so re-producers and unknown authorship are modelled only as the fixed sets; the text-level checks in (W) carry them.

**N4 — Residue.** Closure map item 8 claims "neither a revert nor a further tightening escapes or strands it" (false under B1). Closure map §5's a6ed164 bullet still calls the plan rule "SD-29's stakeholder-chosen narrowing (current revision only)", contradicting the next bullet and the corrected SD-29 row. §31.6 (W)'s discriminator still reads "carry an acceptance past a standing rejection of the current revision". The new §32.2 sentence assigns the inspector a duty ("shows composite-level objections beside records that rely on its parts") that §16.8 does not own.

**N5 — Retained trade-offs and costs.** As 34803df N5 and bf23372 N6: bounded-only reproduction partly reversing SD-17; SD-24's provisional realization; fresh D1/D2 review, acceptance and gate after fences, overtaken re-application and replacement iterations (R4, R12); adverse frame judgments surviving abandonment; accumulating gate minima; the fenced-cutover half state; the context-revert residue; party exclusion from adjudication; the paraphrase-only 2026-10-12 adoption instruction; the empirical limits; human attestation or the owner for equivalence and completeness over unknown-authorship content; an `INCOMPLETE` on a large scope widening every closure inside it. The candidate adds one disclosed cost: the author of the older side of an equivalence cannot qualify it. The revert exemption of the plan row is permissive under the preservation reading (B1) and stakeholder-chosen (SD-29); it belongs with these trade-offs, decided together with B1's options.
